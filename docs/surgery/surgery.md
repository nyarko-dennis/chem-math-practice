# Surgery & Surgical Nursing II — Study Notes (Plain-Language)

A revision companion to the MCQ bank and subjective drills. Every technical term is glossed in everyday words on first use. Aligned with NURS 315 deck material.

---

## How to use this doc

- **MCQs** test recognition and applied reasoning.
- **Drills** test structured exam-style answers (with marking rubrics).
- This document is the connective tissue: it tells you the WHY behind both.
- The plain-language style is deliberate — users said the Pharmacology drills had too much jargon, so here we always teach the word as we use it.

## 1. Oesophagus & Stomach

### Achalasia
- **Plain-words definition:** the ring of muscle at the bottom of the food pipe (the lower oesophageal sphincter, LES) fails to relax, and the food pipe loses its squeezing wave. Food piles up.
- **Symptoms:** progressive trouble swallowing both solids AND liquids; regurgitation of undigested food; night-time cough/aspiration; weight loss.
- **Investigations:** manometry (a thin pressure-measuring tube down the food pipe) = gold standard. Barium swallow shows "bird-beak" tapering. Endoscopy excludes cancer.
- **Surgery:** Heller myotomy (cutting the over-tight muscle); pneumatic balloon dilation; botulinum toxin (temporary).
- **Nursing:** soft/liquid diet pre-op; upright feeding after surgery; watch for perforation and aspiration.

### Oesophageal Varices
- **Plain-words definition:** swollen, thin-walled veins under the lining of the lower food pipe, caused by back-pressure in the portal vein (the vein draining the gut into the liver). Usually from cirrhosis (scarred liver).
- **Bleeding presents with:** large-volume vomiting of bright-red blood; black tarry stools; collapse; signs of shock.
- **Emergency care:** ABC, two large IVs, oxygen, IV fluids/blood, octreotide (lowers portal pressure), urgent endoscopy with band ligation.
- **Long-term:** non-selective beta-blocker (propranolol) prevention; alcohol cessation; treat underlying liver disease.

### Peptic Ulcer Disease (PUD)
- **Plain-words definition:** open sores in the lining of the stomach (gastric ulcer) or first part of the small intestine (duodenal ulcer), caused by acid burning through the lining.
- **Main causes:** H. pylori bacteria (most ulcers); NSAIDs (block protective prostaglandins); smoking, alcohol, severe stress.
- **Pain pattern:** duodenal pain RELIEVED by food (often wakes at night); gastric pain WORSE with food.
- **Diagnosis:** endoscopy with biopsy; H. pylori tests (urea breath test, stool antigen).
- **Treatment:** PPI + triple antibiotics for H. pylori; stop NSAIDs/smoking/alcohol; surgery only for complications (perforation → Graham omental patch; uncontrolled bleeding; outlet obstruction; cancer).

### Gastric Cancer
- **Plain-words definition:** cancer of the stomach (almost always adenocarcinoma).
- **Risk factors:** H. pylori, chronic atrophic gastritis, salty/smoked foods, smoking, pernicious anaemia, BRCA-related genes.
- **Late signs:** weight loss, persistent indigestion, hard left supraclavicular node (Virchow's node), umbilical nodule (Sister Mary Joseph), early satiety.
- **Investigations:** endoscopy + biopsy; staging CT; CA 19-9, CEA.
- **Surgery:** partial or total gastrectomy ± lymphadenectomy; palliative bypass/stenting.
- **Nursing:** nutritional optimisation pre-op; watch for anastomotic leak; teach about dumping syndrome (small low-sugar meals, lie down after eating).

## 2. Liver, Biliary & Pancreas

### Cholelithiasis (Gallstones)
- **Plain-words definition:** stones inside the gallbladder or the tubes that carry bile from the liver to the gut.
- **Risk factors — the "4 Fs":** Female, Fat, Forty, Fertile.
- **Symptoms:** right upper abdominal/epigastric pain after fatty meals, radiating to the right shoulder blade; nausea.
- **Investigations:** ultrasound (gold standard for gallbladder stones); LFTs; MRCP if duct stones suspected.
- **Surgery:** laparoscopic cholecystectomy (gold standard); ERCP for stones in the main bile duct.

### Acute Cholecystitis
- **Plain-words definition:** sudden inflammation of the gallbladder, usually because a stone is blocking the cystic duct (the gallbladder's outflow pipe).
- **Murphy's sign:** the patient catches their breath when the examiner presses under the right rib edge during inspiration.
- **Management:** NPO, IV fluids, antibiotics, analgesia; early laparoscopic cholecystectomy.

### Obstructive Jaundice
- **Plain-words definition:** yellow skin and eyes because bile cannot drain from the liver into the gut. Processed (conjugated) bilirubin backs up into the blood.
- **Classic features:** yellow sclera and skin; dark urine; pale clay-coloured stool; severe itching (bile salts in skin); steatorrhoea (fatty stools); bleeding tendency (vitamin K not absorbed).
- **Painful vs painless:** stone-related = painful, with right upper quadrant colic; cancer-related (pancreatic head) = painless and progressive.
- **Investigations:** LFTs, coagulation, ultrasound, MRCP, ERCP (diagnostic and therapeutic).
- **Pre-op:** parenteral vitamin K to correct clotting.

### Acute Pancreatitis
- **Plain-words definition:** sudden inflammation of the pancreas because its own digestive enzymes are activated early and start digesting the gland (autodigestion).
- **Common causes:** gallstones; alcohol; trauma; ERCP; high triglycerides; hypercalcaemia.
- **Symptoms:** severe upper-abdominal pain boring through to the back; nausea; vomiting; abdominal distension.
- **Severe signs:** Grey-Turner (flank bruising), Cullen (umbilical bruising), hypocalcaemia, ARDS.
- **Investigations:** lipase (more specific than amylase); FBC; U&Es; calcium; LFTs; CRP at 48 h; ultrasound for stones; CT at 48–72 h for severity.
- **Management:** bowel rest (NPO), IV fluids, analgesia, treat cause; antibiotics only if infected necrosis; ERCP for stones.

### Pancreatic Cancer
- **Plain-words definition:** cancer of the pancreas, usually adenocarcinoma of the head of the gland. Aggressive, poor prognosis.
- **Features:** painless progressive jaundice (head); back pain; weight loss; new-onset diabetes; Trousseau's sign (migrating clots).
- **Marker:** CA 19-9.
- **Curative surgery:** Whipple procedure (pancreaticoduodenectomy) — removes head of pancreas, duodenum, part of bile duct, gallbladder, sometimes part of stomach, then reconnects everything. Many post-op risks.

### Anal Fissure
- **Plain-words definition:** a small linear tear in the lining of the anal canal, usually in the back midline, caused by hard stool.
- **Symptoms:** severe tearing pain on passing stool; bright red blood on toilet paper; fear of defecation.
- **Conservative care:** stool softeners, fibre, fluids, sitz baths, topical GTN or anaesthetic.
- **Chronic fissure:** lateral internal sphincterotomy.

## 3. Bowel & Anorectal

### Hernias
- **Plain-words definition:** a bulge of organ or tissue through a weak spot or natural opening in the wall of the cavity that normally holds it.
- **Parts:** opening (defect), sac (pouch of peritoneum), contents (bowel, omentum), coverings.
- **Types:**
  - **Indirect inguinal:** through deep inguinal ring → inguinal canal → may enter scrotum; usually congenital (patent processus vaginalis).
  - **Direct inguinal:** straight through weak posterior wall; older men, acquired.
  - **Femoral:** through femoral canal in upper thigh; more common in women; high strangulation risk.
  - **Umbilical, incisional, epigastric, hiatus, obturator.**
- **Status:** reducible, irreducible/incarcerated, obstructed, strangulated (emergency — blood supply cut off).
- **Repair:** Lichtenstein mesh (open) or laparoscopic mesh; femoral hernias always repaired.

### Intestinal Obstruction
- **Plain-words definition:** the gut contents can't pass — either because of a physical block (mechanical) or because the gut has stopped moving (paralytic ileus).
- **Mechanical causes:** adhesions (most common in adults), hernia, tumour, volvulus, intussusception.
- **Symptoms:** colicky pain, vomiting, distension, no flatus/stool.
- **Management ("drip and suck"):** NPO, NG tube, IV fluids and electrolytes, monitor urine output; surgery if not resolving or signs of strangulation.

### Acute Appendicitis
- **Plain-words definition:** sudden inflammation of the appendix.
- **Pain pattern:** vague central pain → SHIFTS to right iliac fossa (McBurney's point).
- **Signs:** rebound tenderness, Rovsing's sign (left-press, right-pain), low-grade fever.
- **Avoid laxatives and heating pads** — both can cause perforation.
- **Treatment:** appendicectomy, open or laparoscopic.

### Haemorrhoids
- **Plain-words definition:** swollen vascular cushions inside (internal) or outside (external) the anal canal.
- **Internal grading:** I bleed only; II prolapse and reduce; III need manual reduction; IV cannot be reduced.
- **Symptoms:** painless bright-red bleeding on stool (internal); pain only with thrombosis or external.
- **Conservative care:** high fibre, fluids, sitz baths, stool softeners.
- **Procedures:** rubber band ligation; sclerotherapy; haemorrhoidectomy.

### Colorectal Cancer
- **Plain-words definition:** cancer arising from the lining of the colon or rectum (usually adenocarcinoma from a polyp).
- **Right-sided:** iron-deficiency anaemia from slow bleeding, fatigue, vague mass.
- **Left-sided:** change in bowel habit, narrow stools, blood mixed with stool, obstruction.
- **Rectal:** tenesmus (constant urge), bright bleeding.
- **Investigations:** colonoscopy + biopsy (gold standard); CEA for monitoring; staging CT.
- **Surgery:** depends on site (right or left hemicolectomy, sigmoid colectomy, anterior resection); may involve colostomy.
- **Stoma care:** healthy stoma is pink/red, moist; dusky = ischaemia.

### Rectal Prolapse
- **Plain-words definition:** the rectum slides down and protrudes through the anus.
- **Causes:** pelvic floor weakness, chronic straining, multiparity, ageing.
- **Surgery:** rectopexy (abdominal); Delorme or Altemeier procedure (perineal — for frail patients).

## 4. Paediatric & Bariatric Surgery

### Infantile Hypertrophic Pyloric Stenosis
- **Plain-words definition:** the muscle at the stomach's outlet (pylorus) is too thick, so milk can't empty.
- **Classic presentation:** 3–6 week old male, projectile NON-BILIOUS vomiting; "olive" mass in epigastrium; still hungry after vomiting; weight loss; dehydration.
- **Electrolytes:** hypochloraemic, hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis.
- **Diagnosis:** abdominal ultrasound shows thickened pylorus.
- **Treatment:** correct fluids and electrolytes FIRST, then Ramstedt pyloromyotomy (split the muscle).

### Bariatric Surgery
- **Indications:** BMI ≥40, or ≥35 with co-morbidities (T2DM, OSA, HTN).
- **Procedures:**
  - **Sleeve gastrectomy:** remove ~80% of stomach (restrictive; reduces ghrelin).
  - **Roux-en-Y gastric bypass:** small pouch + bypass; restrictive AND malabsorptive; powerful for T2DM.
  - **Gastric banding:** adjustable band (now less common).
- **Complications:** leak (early — tachycardia is the first sign), VTE, dumping syndrome, nutritional deficiencies (lifelong supplements: multivitamin, B12, iron, calcium, vitamin D), late hypoglycaemia, weight regain.
- **Diet progression:** clear liquids → full liquids → pureed → soft → small frequent meals over 6–8 weeks.

## 5. Cardiothoracic

### Aortic Aneurysm
- **Plain-words definition:** an abnormal localised ballooning of the aorta (the body's main artery) because of wall weakness.
- **Risk:** atherosclerosis + hypertension + smoking; Marfan/Ehlers-Danlos; advanced age, male.
- **AAA signs:** pulsatile abdominal mass; rupture = sudden pain + shock + mass.
- **Investigations:** ultrasound (screening), CT angiography.
- **Repair:** elective at ≥5.5 cm or growing fast; open repair vs EVAR (endovascular stent graft via femoral artery).
- **Ruptured AAA:** emergency surgery; permissive hypotension until clamped.

### Aortic Dissection
- **Plain-words definition:** a tear in the inner wall of the aorta lets blood split the wall layers, creating a false channel.
- **Pain:** sudden "tearing/ripping" chest pain radiating to back.
- **Stanford classification:** Type A (ascending) = emergency surgery; Type B (descending) = medical management.
- **First-line drug:** IV beta-blocker (labetalol/esmolol) to control BP AND heart rate (lowers shear stress).
- **Mortality without treatment:** 1–2% per hour.

### Atrial Myxoma
- **Plain-words definition:** the most common benign primary heart tumour, usually pedunculated (on a stalk) in the left atrium.
- **Triad of effects:** Obstruction (mimics mitral stenosis), Embolisation (stroke), Constitutional symptoms (fever, weight loss).
- **Diagnosis:** echocardiography.
- **Treatment:** surgical excision with cardiopulmonary bypass; usually curative in sporadic cases.

### Lung Cancer
- **Plain-words definition:** malignant tumour from the lung lining (bronchi, bronchioles, alveoli).
- **Types:** Non-small cell (NSCLC, 85% — adeno, squamous, large cell); Small cell (SCLC, aggressive).
- **Risk:** smoking (biggest factor), passive smoke, asbestos, radon, pollution.
- **Symptoms:** persistent cough, haemoptysis, dyspnoea, chest pain; hoarseness (recurrent laryngeal nerve compression); SVC syndrome (face/neck swelling, distended veins).
- **Investigations:** CXR, CT, biopsy (bronchoscopy for central, CT-guided for peripheral), PET.
- **Surgery:** lobectomy (most common), pneumonectomy, wedge/segmentectomy; lymph node dissection.
- **Pre-op:** smoking cessation; treat infections; breathing exercises and incentive spirometry.

### Mediastinal Masses
- **Anterior mediastinum "4 Ts":** Thymoma, Teratoma (germ cell), Thyroid, Terrible lymphoma.
- **Thymoma:** linked to myasthenia gravis; thymectomy can improve symptoms.
- **Mediastinal lymphoma:** Hodgkin vs non-Hodgkin; treated with chemo ± radio; biopsy for tissue diagnosis.
- **Germ cell tumours:** AFP and β-hCG; seminomas (β-hCG only) vs non-seminomatous.

### Pleural Effusion & Empyema
- **Pleural effusion:** fluid in the pleural space (the gap between lung and chest wall).
- **Empyema:** pus in the pleural space (infected effusion).
- **Empyema stages:** (1) exudative — clear fluid; (2) fibrinopurulent — pus and loculations; (3) organising — fibrous "peel" traps the lung.
- **Recurrent effusion causes:** malignancy, heart failure, cirrhosis, renal failure, TB.
- **Treatment:** chest tube drainage; VATS / decortication for late-stage empyema; pleurodesis for recurrent effusion.
- **Chest tube care:**
  - Drainage bottle BELOW chest level.
  - Tidaling = working; continuous bubbling = air leak.
  - Maintain semi-Fowler's position; aseptic dressing changes.
  - Remove when drainage minimal, no air leak, lung re-expanded, patient stable.

## 6. Thoracic Emergencies

### Tension Pneumothorax
- **Plain-words definition:** air enters the pleural space with each breath and can't escape; pressure builds, lung collapses, mediastinum shifts AWAY, venous return drops → shock.
- **Signs:** severe dyspnoea, tachycardia, hypotension, distended neck veins, hyperresonance, absent breath sounds, tracheal deviation (late).
- **CLINICAL diagnosis** — do NOT delay for X-ray.
- **Treatment:** immediate needle decompression (2nd ICS midclavicular OR 5th ICS midaxillary) → chest tube.

### Flail Chest
- **Plain-words definition:** ≥3 consecutive ribs each broken in ≥2 places, so a free segment moves paradoxically (inward) with inspiration.
- **Hypoxia mainly from:** underlying pulmonary contusion.
- **Treatment:** oxygen, effective pain control, mechanical ventilation if respiratory failure; rib fixation in selected cases.

### Massive Haemothorax
- **Plain-words definition:** >1500 mL blood in pleural space initially, or >200 mL/hour ongoing.
- **Signs:** dyspnoea, hypotension, tachycardia, DULL percussion, decreased breath sounds.
- **Treatment:** two large-bore IVs, crossmatched blood, chest tube, emergency thoracotomy if bleeding continues.

## 7. Urology & Andrology

### BPH (Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia)
- **Plain-words definition:** non-cancerous enlargement of the prostate squeezing the urethra.
- **Obstructive symptoms:** weak stream, hesitancy, straining, dribbling, incomplete emptying.
- **Irritative symptoms:** frequency, nocturia, urgency.
- **Investigations:** DRE (smoothly enlarged), urinalysis, PSA, bladder scan (post-void residual), U&Es; cystoscopy if surgery planned.
- **Treatment:** lifestyle, alpha-blockers (tamsulosin), 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors (finasteride); TURP for failed medical therapy.
- **Post-TURP:** continuous bladder irrigation; watch for haemorrhage and TUR syndrome (dilutional hyponatraemia).

### Prostate Cancer
- **DRE:** hard, nodular, irregular.
- **Risk:** age, African ancestry, BRCA, family history.
- **Spread:** osteoblastic bone metastases (back, pelvis).
- **PSA:** screening + monitoring (interpret with caution).
- **Treatment:** active surveillance vs radical prostatectomy vs radiotherapy vs hormonal (LHRH agonist/antagonist) ± chemotherapy.

### Urinary Retention
- **Acute:** sudden tense painful bladder. Catheterise immediately.
- **Causes:** BPH (commonest in men), strictures, drugs (anticholinergics, opioids), post-op, neuropathy.

### Bladder Cancer
- **Most common type:** transitional (urothelial) cell carcinoma.
- **Schistosoma haematobium** → squamous cell carcinoma in endemic regions.
- **Classic feature:** painless gross haematuria.
- **Diagnosis:** cystoscopy + biopsy (gold standard); CT urogram; urine cytology.
- **Treatment:** TURBT ± intravesical chemo (BCG/mitomycin); partial or radical cystectomy.

### Varicocele
- **Plain-words definition:** dilated pampiniform veins ("bag of worms"), usually left-sided (left testicular vein drains at right angles into renal vein).
- **Sudden right-sided varicocele in adult** → look for renal/IVC mass.
- **Affects fertility** by raising testicular temperature.

### Nephrolithiasis (Kidney Stones)
- **Pain:** "loin-to-groin" colicky flank pain ± haematuria, nausea.
- **Investigation of choice:** non-contrast CT KUB.
- **Treatment:** hydration, analgesia, alpha-blockers; ESWL (sound-wave shock), ureteroscopy, PCNL (percutaneous nephrolithotomy).

### Testicular Torsion
- **Plain-words definition:** twisting of the spermatic cord → loss of blood supply.
- **Window:** save the testis within ~6 hours.
- **Signs:** sudden severe pain, swollen high-riding testis, absent cremasteric reflex.
- **Treatment:** emergency exploration → detorsion + bilateral orchidopexy; orchidectomy if non-viable.

### Hydrocele
- **Plain-words definition:** painless fluid collection around the testis inside the tunica vaginalis.
- **Sign:** positive transillumination.
- **Treatment:** hydrocelectomy.

### Cryptorchidism (Undescended Testis)
- **Treatment:** orchidopexy before 1–2 years to protect fertility and allow future cancer screening.

## 8. Breast & Gynaecology

### Breast Cancer
- **Plain-words definition:** malignant proliferation of ductal or lobular epithelial cells.
- **Risk:** female sex, age, BRCA1/2, early menarche/late menopause, nulliparity, obesity, alcohol.
- **Signs of concern:** hard fixed irregular lump; skin dimpling; peau d'orange; nipple retraction; bloody nipple discharge; axillary lymphadenopathy.
- **Diagnosis:** mammography (screening), ultrasound, core biopsy (definitive), ER/PR/HER2.
- **Surgery:** lumpectomy + radiotherapy (breast-conserving); modified radical mastectomy ± reconstruction; sentinel node biopsy vs axillary clearance.
- **Drains:** Jackson-Pratt/Redivac under skin flaps.
- **Lymphoedema prevention:** lifelong precautions on operated arm.

### Cervical Cancer
- **Plain-words definition:** malignant growth of the cervix, almost always HPV-related (especially HPV 16, 18).
- **Symptoms:** postcoital bleeding, foul-smelling discharge, irregular bleeding, pelvic pain.
- **Screening:** Pap smear (cervical cytology), HPV DNA testing, colposcopy + biopsy.
- **Surgery:** cone biopsy (early), trachelectomy (fertility-sparing), radical hysterectomy + pelvic LN dissection.

### Ectopic Pregnancy
- **Plain-words definition:** fertilised ovum implants outside the uterus, usually in the fallopian tube.
- **Risks:** previous ectopic, PID, tubal surgery, IUD use, IVF, smoking, endometriosis.
- **Symptoms:** missed period, unilateral lower abdominal pain, vaginal spotting, shoulder-tip pain (diaphragm irritation), shock (if ruptured).
- **Diagnosis:** beta-hCG (serial; slow rise suggests ectopic) + transvaginal ultrasound; laparoscopy is gold standard.
- **Treatment:** methotrexate (stable, small, unruptured); salpingostomy or salpingectomy (surgery); emergency laparotomy if ruptured + shocked.
- **Anti-D** if Rh-negative.

### Imperforate Hymen
- **Plain-words definition:** congenital obstruction of the vaginal opening from failure of vaginal plate canalisation.
- **Presentation:** primary amenorrhoea (no periods) despite breast development; cyclic pelvic pain; bluish bulging membrane; haematocolpos.
- **Treatment:** hymenectomy with cruciate incision; sitz baths; teaching.

### Bartholin's Cyst / Abscess
- **Plain-words definition:** blocked Bartholin duct (lubricating glands at vaginal opening) → cyst; if infected → abscess.
- **Treatment:** incision & drainage with Word catheter; marsupialisation (suture cyst open); excision for recurrent.

### Ovarian Cyst, Torsion, Cancer
- **Functional cyst:** most cysts in reproductive age — usually resolve.
- **Ovarian torsion:** sudden severe pelvic pain; emergency surgical detorsion ± oophorectomy if necrotic.
- **Ovarian cancer:** late diagnosis common; vague bloating, early satiety, urinary frequency; CA-125 + ultrasound; TAH + BSO + omentectomy + debulking + chemo.

### Endometrial Cancer
- **Classic feature:** postmenopausal bleeding.
- **Diagnosis:** endometrial biopsy (gold standard); transvaginal ultrasound; MRI for staging.
- **Treatment:** total hysterectomy + BSO + lymph node dissection.

### Uterine Leiomyoma (Fibroids)
- **Plain-words definition:** benign smooth-muscle tumours of the uterus, oestrogen- and progesterone-dependent.
- **Higher prevalence and severity in women of African descent.**
- **Types:** intramural (commonest), submucosal (bleeding/fertility), subserosal (pressure), pedunculated (torsion), cervical.
- **Symptoms:** heavy bleeding, pressure symptoms, anaemia, infertility.
- **Medical:** tranexamic acid, NSAIDs, COCs, progestogens, GnRH agonists (shrink fibroids pre-op).
- **Minimally invasive:** uterine artery embolisation (UAE); MRI-guided focused ultrasound (MRgFUS).
- **Surgery:** myomectomy (fertility-preserving) vs hysterectomy (definitive). Conserve ovaries if pre-menopausal.

## Cross-cutting Nursing Principles

### Pre-operative
- Focused history and examination; baseline observations.
- Investigations (FBC, U&Es, LFTs, coagulation, group & save); ECG/CXR as indicated.
- Treat anaemia and malnutrition; optimise diabetes and hypertension.
- Stop smoking; treat chest infections.
- VTE prophylaxis (TED stockings + LMWH).
- Informed written consent.
- Pre-op antibiotics 60 minutes before incision.
- Mark surgical site.
- WHO Surgical Safety Checklist.
- Emotional support, family involvement, patient education.

### Post-operative
- ABC, vital signs, oxygen saturation.
- Pain control — multimodal (paracetamol + NSAID + opioid ± regional).
- Fluid balance and electrolytes; urine output ≥0.5 mL/kg/h.
- Wound and drain care (asepsis).
- Early mobilisation, deep breathing, incentive spirometry.
- VTE prophylaxis continued.
- Watch for complications: bleeding, infection, anastomotic leak, ileus, DVT/PE, atelectasis, pneumonia.
- Nutrition: staged progression as appropriate.
- Discharge teaching: wound care, medications, activity, warning signs, follow-up.

### Emergency Mindset
- Recognise the picture early — many emergencies are CLINICAL diagnoses.
- ABC always first.
- Two large-bore IVs for any unstable patient.
- Group and crossmatch early.
- Communicate clearly with the team; document precisely.
- Keep the patient warm; avoid the lethal triad of hypothermia + acidosis + coagulopathy.

## Quick recall lists

**4 Ts of anterior mediastinum:** Thymoma, Teratoma (germ cell), Thyroid, Terrible lymphoma.
**4 Fs of gallstones:** Female, Fat, Forty, Fertile.
**Charcot's triad (cholangitis):** RUQ pain + fever + jaundice.
**Beck's triad (tamponade):** hypotension + muffled heart sounds + distended neck veins.
**Triad of atrial myxoma:** Obstruction + Embolisation + Constitutional symptoms.
**Stanford A vs B aortic dissection:** A = ascending = surgery; B = descending = medical (usually).
**Cushing vs Curling ulcer:** Cushing — head injury; Curling — burns.
**Virchow's node:** left supraclavicular — gastric (and other intra-abdominal) cancer spread.
**Sister Mary Joseph nodule:** periumbilical mass — intra-abdominal malignancy.
