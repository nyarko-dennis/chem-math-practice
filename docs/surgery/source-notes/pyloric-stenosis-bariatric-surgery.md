## Slide 2

Pyloric Stenosis
Definition
Pyloric stenosis is a condition characterized by hypertrophy and hyperplasia of the pyloric muscle, leading to gastric outlet obstruction, most commonly seen in infants (infantile hypertrophic pyloric stenosis).

## Slide 3

Aetiology/Risk Factors
Infantile (most common)
Idiopathic
Genetic predisposition (family history)
Male sex (more common in males)
First-born child
Macrolide antibiotic exposure (e.g., erythromycin)
Adult pyloric stenosis
Chronic Peptic Ulcer Disease (scarring)
Gastric tumors
Chronic gastritis

## Slide 4

Pathophysiology
Hypertrophy of pyloric circular muscle
Narrowing of pyloric canal
Gastric emptying is obstructed
Food accumulates in stomach → dilation
Persistent vomiting → dehydration and electrolyte imbalance
Electrolyte imbalance:
Hypochloremic, hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis

## Slide 5

Clinical Features
In infants
Projectile, non-bilious vomiting (key feature)
Visible gastric peristalsis
Palpable “olive-shaped” mass in epigastrium
Persistent hunger after vomiting
Weight loss or failure to thrive
Dehydration
In adults
Nausea, vomiting
Early satiety
Epigastric fullness
Weight loss

## Slide 6

Investigations
Abdominal ultrasound (gold standard)
Thickened pylorus
Serum electrolytes (alkalosis)
Barium study (“string sign”)
Endoscopy (adults)

## Slide 7

Complications
Severe dehydration
Electrolyte imbalance
Malnutrition
Gastric perforation (rare)

## Slide 8

Surgical Management
Ramstedt pyloromyotomy (definitive treatment in infants)
Splitting of hypertrophied muscle without removing it
For adults:
Treat underlying cause
Pyloroplasty or gastrectomy (if severe)

## Slide 9

Nursing Management
Pre-operative
Correct dehydration (IV fluids)
Correct electrolyte imbalance
Monitor: Urine output
Keep patient NPO
Nasogastric tube for decompression
Post-operative
Monitor for vomiting (should reduce)
Gradual feeding:Small, frequent feeds
Monitor surgical site
Continue fluid/electrolyte balance
Parent education (in infants):
Feeding techniques
Signs of complications

## Slide 10

Bariatric Surgery
Bariatric surgery refers to surgical procedures performed to induce weight loss in individuals with obesity by restricting food intake and/or reducing nutrient absorption.

## Slide 11

Indications
BMI ≥40 kg/m²
BMI ≥35 kg/m² with comorbidities:
Diabetes mellitus
Hypertension
Obstructive sleep apnea
Failed conservative management (diet, exercise, drugs)

## Slide 12

Types of Bariatric Surgery
Restrictive procedures
Sleeve gastrectomy
Adjustable gastric band
Malabsorptive procedures
Biliopancreatic diversion
Combined procedures
Roux-en-Y gastric bypass (most common)

## Slide 13

Pathophysiology
Reduced stomach size → early satiety
Altered gut hormones (↓ ghrelin, ↑ GLP-1)
Reduced nutrient absorption (in bypass)
Improved insulin sensitivity

## Slide 14

Signs and Outcomes
Significant weight loss
Improvement or resolution of:
Type 2 diabetes
Hypertension
Dyslipidemia
Improved quality of life

## Slide 15

Investigations(Pre-op Work up)
BMI calculation
Blood tests: FBC, LFTs, glucose
Nutritional assessment
Psychological evaluation
Endoscopy (if indicated)

## Slide 16

Complications
Early
Bleeding
Infection
Anastomotic leak
Pulmonary embolism
Late
Nutritional deficiencies:
Iron
Vitamin B12
Calcium
Dumping syndrome
Bowel obstruction
Weight regain

## Slide 17

Surgical Procedures
1. Sleeve Gastrectomy
Removal of ~80% of stomach
Restrictive
2. Roux-en-Y Gastric Bypass
Small gastric pouch created
Bypasses part of small intestine
3. Gastric Banding
Adjustable band placed around stomach

## Slide 18

Nursing Management
Pre-operative
Patient education:Lifestyle changes
Lifelong follow-up
Nutritional counseling
Psychological preparation
Baseline investigations
DVT prophylaxis
Post-operative
Immediate:
Monitor:Vital signs,Oxygen saturation,Signs of leakage (tachycardia, fever)
Pain management
Early ambulation (prevent DVT)

## Slide 19

Nursing Management Contd
Nutritional Care Stage diet progression:
Clear liquids, Full liquids, Pureed diet, Soft diet, Small frequent meals. Avoid high-sugar foods
Long-term Care
Vitamin supplementation:
IronB12, Calcium.
Monitor weight loss
Encourage lifestyle modification
Patient EducationEat slowly, chew thoroughly
Avoid overeating
Lifelong follow-up required
Recognize complications (vomiting, dizziness)

## Slide 20

Summary
Pyloric stenosis → obstruction due to pyloric muscle hypertrophy → projectile vomiting in infants
Bariatric surgery → weight-loss surgery for obesity → works via restriction and/or malabsorption
