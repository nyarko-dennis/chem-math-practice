## Slide 3

Achalasia
Definition
A primary esophageal motility disorder characterized by:
Failure of LES relaxation
Absence of peristalsis

## Slide 4

Aetiology/Risk Factors
Idiopathic (most common)
Degeneration of myenteric plexus
Autoimmune or viral causes

## Slide 5

Pathophysiology
Loss of inhibitory neurons
LES remains contracted
Food accumulates → esophageal dilation → stasis

## Slide 6

Clinical Features
Progressive dysphagia (solids → liquids)
Regurgitation of undigested food
Chest pain
Weight loss
Nocturnal cough (aspiration)

## Slide 7

Investigations
Esophageal manometry (gold standard)
Barium swallow: “Bird-beak” narrowing
Endoscopy (exclude cancer)

## Slide 8

Complications
Aspiration pneumonia
Megaesophagus
Esophageal carcinoma

## Slide 9

Surgical Management
Heller myotomy
Pneumatic balloon dilation
Botulinum toxin injection

## Slide 10

Nursing Management
Pre-operative:
Nutritional assessment
Encourage soft/liquid diet
Educate on procedure
Post-operative:
Monitor for perforation
Gradual diet progression
Upright feeding position
Aspiration precautions

## Slide 11

Esophageal Varices
Definition
Dilated submucosal veins in the esophagus due to portal hypertension, prone to rupture and life-threatening bleeding.

## Slide 12

Aetiology/Risk Factors
Liver cirrhosis (most common)
Chronic alcohol use
Viral hepatitis (B, C)
Portal vein thrombosis

## Slide 13

Pathophysiology
Portal hypertension develops
Blood diverted to collateral pathways
Esophageal veins dilate
Increased pressure → rupture → massive bleeding

## Slide 14

Clinical Features
Hematemesis (massive, bright red)
Melena
Hypotension
Tachycardia
Signs of liver disease: Jaundice, Ascites, Spider nevi

## Slide 15

Investigations
Endoscopy (diagnostic & therapeutic)
LFTs
Coagulation profile
Ultrasound (portal system)

## Slide 16

Complications
Massive hemorrhage
Hypovolemic shock
Hepatic encephalopathy
Death

## Slide 17

Surgical Management
Emergency
IV fluids + blood transfusion
Vasopressors (e.g., octreotide)
Procedures
Endoscopic band ligation (first line)
Sclerotherapy
Balloon tamponade (temporary)
TIPS (shunt procedure)

## Slide 18

Nursing Management
Pre-operative / Emergency
ABC resuscitation
Oxygen therapy
Insert IV lines (large bore)
Monitor vitals continuously
Prepare for urgent endoscopy
Post-operative
Monitor for rebleeding
Maintain airway
Administer prescribed drugs
Prevent aspiration
Patient education:
Avoid alcohol
Adhere to liver disease management

## Slide 19

Peptic Ulcer Disease
Definition
Peptic ulcer disease refers to discrete mucosal erosions ≥5 mm in the stomach or proximal duodenum that penetrate the muscularis mucosa due to acid-peptic injury.

## Slide 20

Aetiology/Risk Factors
Infectious
Helicobacter pylori (causes ~90% duodenal and 70% gastric ulcers)
Drug-induced
NSAIDs (inhibit prostaglandin synthesis → mucosal damage)
Corticosteroids (especially with NSAIDs)
Lifestyle
Smoking (impairs healing)
Alcohol (irritates mucosa)
Caffeine (↑ acid secretion)
Others
Zollinger–Ellison syndrome (gastrin-secreting tumor)
Severe physiological stress (burns, trauma → Curling ulcers)

## Slide 21

Pathophysiology
Normal balance:
Aggressive factors: HCl, pepsin, H. pylori
Protective factors: mucus, bicarbonate, prostaglandins, mucosal blood flow
Disease process:
H. pylori colonizes gastric mucosa → urease production → ammonia → mucosal injury
NSAIDs ↓ prostaglandins → ↓ mucus & bicarbonate
Increased acid penetrates mucosa → inflammation → ulceration

## Slide 22

Clinical Features
Epigastric pain:
Burning/gnawing
Duodenal - relieved by meals
Gastric - worsens with meals
Nocturnal pain (common in duodenal ulcers)
Nausea, vomiting
Bloating, early satiety
GI bleeding: Hematemesis
Melena
Severe cases:
Sudden severe abdominal pain (perforation)

## Slide 23

Investigations
Upper GI endoscopy (gold standard)
Visualizes ulcer + allows biopsy
H. pylori tests:
Urea breath test
Stool antigen test
Rapid urease test
FBC (anemia from bleeding)
Serum gastrin (if Zollinger–Ellison suspected)

## Slide 24

Complications
Hemorrhage (most common)
Perforation → peritonitis
Penetration (into pancreas/liver)
Gastric outlet obstruction (from edema/scarring)

## Slide 25

Surgical Management
Indications:
Failure of medical therapy
Complications
Procedures:
Vagotomy (↓ acid secretion)
Antrectomy / partial gastrectomy
Graham patch repair (perforation)

## Slide 26

Nursing Management
Pre-operative
Monitor vitals and signs of shock
Assess for GI bleeding
Maintain NPO
Administer:
Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs)
IV fluids
Prepare for endoscopy/surgery
Post-operative
Monitor:
Vital signs
Drain output
Signs of leakage
Pain management
Gradual feeding (clear → soft diet)
Patient education:
Avoid NSAIDs, smoking, alcohol
Complete H. pylori therapy

## Slide 27

Gastric Cancer
Definition
A malignant neoplasm of the stomach, most commonly adenocarcinoma arising from the gastric mucosa.

## Slide 28

Aetiology/Risk Factors
H. pylori infection
Chronic atrophic gastritis
Diet: Smoked, salted foods, Low fruits/vegetables
Smoking
Genetic factors (e.g., CDH1 mutation)
Pernicious anemia
Previous gastric surgery

## Slide 29

Pathophysiology
Chronic inflammation → mucosal atrophy
Intestinal metaplasia → dysplasia
Malignant transformation
Spread:
Direct invasion (stomach wall)
Lymphatic spread (Virchow’s node)
Hematogenous (liver)
Peritoneal dissemination

## Slide 30

Clinical Features
Early
Often asymptomatic
Late
Weight loss
Anorexia
Persistent epigastric pain
Nausea/vomiting
Dysphagia (proximal tumors)
Hematemesis/melena
Signs:
Virchow’s node (left supraclavicular)
Sister Mary Joseph nodule

## Slide 31

Investigations
Endoscopy + biopsy (definitive)
CT scan (staging)
Endoscopic ultrasound
Tumor markers (CEA, CA 19-9)

## Slide 32

Complications
Metastasis
Obstruction
Bleeding
Cachexia

## Slide 33

Surgical Management
Curative
Partial gastrectomy
Total gastrectomy
Lymphadenectomy
Palliative
Bypass surgery
Stenting
Adjunct:
Chemotherapy
Radiotherapy

## Slide 34

Nursing Management
Pre-operative
Nutritional optimization
Correct anemia
Psychological support
Pre-op education
Post-operative
Monitor for:
Anastomotic leak
Infection
NG tube management
Nutritional care:
Small frequent meals
Prevent dumping syndrome:
Low sugar diet
Lie down after meals

## Slide 35

Summary
PUD → imbalance of acid vs mucosal defense
Gastric cancer → chronic inflammation → malignancy
Achalasia → LES fails to relax → dysphagia
Esophageal varices → portal hypertension → fatal bleeding risk
