## Slide 3

Atrial Myxoma
An Atrial myxoma is a benign (non-cancerous) tumor of the heart that usually develops in the atria, most commonly the left atrium.
It is the most common primary tumor of the heart in adults.
The tumor is typically:
Soft
Gelatinous
Pedunculated (attached by a stalk)
Mobile within the atrium
Because it moves with blood flow, it may block the mitral valve opening or break off and cause embolism.

## Slide 4

Aetiology/Risk Factors
The exact cause of atrial myxoma is not fully known, but several factors are associated.
Sporadic cases
Most atrial myxomas occur sporadically without a clear cause.
Genetic conditions
Some occur as part of familial syndromes, especially:
Carney Complex:
A rare inherited disorder
Associated with multiple myxomas
Also causes endocrine tumors and skin pigmentation abnormalities
Risk Factors
Family history
Middle age
Female gender (slightly more common)
Genetic syndromes

## Slide 5

Pathophysiology
Atrial myxoma develops from mesenchymal cells in the endocardium (inner lining of the heart).
Step-by-step mechanism
Abnormal cell growth begins in the atrial endocardium.
These cells multiply and form a gelatinous tumor mass.
As the tumor enlarges:
It may move with blood flow inside the atrium.
During diastole,
it may prolapse into the mitral valve opening.
This creates intermittent obstruction to blood flow
from the left atrium to the left ventricle.

## Slide 6

Pathophysiology Contd
Effects on circulation
The tumor can cause three major problems:
1. ObstructionThe tumor blocks blood flow through the valve, mimicking mitral stenosis.
2. EmbolizationPieces of the tumor or blood clots can break off and travel through circulation.
This can lead to:
Stroke
Peripheral embolism
Organ infarction
3. Systemic inflammatory responseMyxomas produce cytokines, causing systemic symptoms such as fever and fatigue.

## Slide 7

Clinical Features
Obstructive Symptoms
These occur when the tumor blocks blood flow.
Examples:
Shortness of breath
Dizziness
Fainting (syncope)
Orthopnea
Paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea
Fatigue
These symptoms resemble mitral valve disease.
SAK-NURS 315

## Slide 8

Clinical Features Contd
Embolic Symptoms
Fragments may travel through the bloodstream.
Possible effects include:
Stroke
Transient ischemic attack
Limb ischemia
Kidney infarction
Retinal artery embolism
About 30–40% of patients develop embolic events.

## Slide 9

Clinical Features Contd
Constitutional Symptoms
These occur due to inflammatory substances produced by the tumor.
Examples:
Fever
Weight loss
Fatigue
Joint pain
Malaise

## Slide 10

Investigations
Several diagnostic tests help confirm atrial myxoma.
Echocardiography (Most important)
Transthoracic echocardiography can show:
Location of tumor
Size
Mobility
Valve obstruction
Transesophageal echocardiography
Provides more detailed images of cardiac structures.
CT Scan
Used to visualize the tumor and surrounding structures.
MRI
Provides highly detailed images of cardiac masses.
ECG
May show:
Atrial enlargement
Arrhythmias
Chest X-ray
May show signs of heart enlargement or pulmonary congestion.
SAK-NURS 315

## Slide 11

Diagnosis
Diagnosis is primarily based on cardiac imaging, especially echocardiography.
Doctors evaluate:
Tumor location
Size
Mobility
Risk of embolization
Early diagnosis is important because surgical removal is curative in most cases.
SAK-NURS 315

## Slide 12

Complications
If untreated, atrial myxoma can cause serious complications.
Systemic embolism
Fragments travel to organs causing infarction.
Stroke
One of the most serious complications.
Heart failure
Due to obstruction of blood flow through the mitral valve.
Arrhythmias
Abnormal heart rhythms may occur.
Sudden cardiac death
Rare but possible if the tumor blocks blood flow completely.
SAK-NURS 315

## Slide 13

Surgical Management
Surgical removal is the definitive treatment.
The procedure is called open heart tumor excision.
Procedure overview
Patient undergoes open heart surgery.
Cardiopulmonary bypass is used.
The tumor and its stalk are completely removed.
The atrial wall is repaired.
Sometimes the interatrial septum is partially removed to prevent recurrence.

Surgical Management
Prognosis
After surgery:
Symptoms resolve quickly

## Slide 17

Summary
Atrial myxoma is the most common primary benign heart tumor.
It usually develops in the left atrium.
Major clinical effects include:
Obstruction of blood flow
Embolism
Systemic inflammatory symptoms
Diagnosis is mainly through echocardiography.
Surgical removal is the definitive treatment.
Nursing care is essential in preoperative preparation and postoperative recovery.
