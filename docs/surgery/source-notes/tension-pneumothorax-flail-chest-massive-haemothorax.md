## Slide 3

Tension Pneumothorax
A life-threatening condition in which air enters the pleural space and cannot escape, leading to increased intrathoracic pressure, lung collapse, and mediastinal shift.

## Slide 4

Aetiology/Risk Factors
Penetrating chest trauma
Blunt chest trauma
Mechanical ventilation (barotrauma)
Ruptured bleb (spontaneous)

## Slide 5

Pathophysiology
Air enters pleural space during inspiration but cannot exit
Progressive accumulation of air increases pressure
Ipsilateral lung collapses
Mediastinum shifts to opposite side
Compression of great vessels reduces venous return → shock

## Slide 6

Clinical Features
Severe dyspnea
Tachycardia
Hypotension
Distended neck veins (JVD)
Tracheal deviation (late sign)
Hyperresonance on percussion
Absent breath sounds on affected side

## Slide 7

Investigations
Clinical diagnosis (do not delay treatment)
Chest X-ray (if stable)
Ultrasound (FAST)
ABG (hypoxia)

## Slide 8

Complications
Cardiac arrest
Respiratory failure
Death

## Slide 9

Surgical Management
Immediate needle decompression (2nd ICS midclavicular or 5th ICS midaxillary)
Chest tube (tube thoracostomy)

## Slide 10

Nursing Management
Pre-operative
Rapid ABC assessment
Administer high-flow oxygen
Prepare for emergency decompression
Monitor vitals continuously
Post-operative
Monitor chest tube function
Assess respiratory status
Pain management
Prevent infection

## Slide 11

Flail Chest
A condition where a segment of the chest wall becomes detached due to multiple rib fractures, causing paradoxical movement.

## Slide 12

Aetiology/Risk Factors
Road traffic accidents
Falls
Assault
Elderly (osteoporosis)

## Slide 13

Pathophysiology
≥3 ribs fractured in ≥2 places
Free-floating segment moves opposite to normal breathing
Ineffective ventilation
Often associated with pulmonary contusion → hypoxia

## Slide 14

Clinical Features
Paradoxical chest movement
Chest pain
Dyspnea
Tachypnea
Cyanosis (severe)

## Slide 15

Investigations
Chest X-ray
CT scan
ABG

## Slide 16

Complications
Respiratory failure
Pneumonia
ARDS

## Slide 17

Management
Oxygen therapy
Mechanical ventilation
Surgical rib fixation (selected cases)

## Slide 18

Nursing Care
Pre-operative
Oxygen therapy
Pain control
Positioning (semi-Fowler’s)
Monitor vitals
Post-operative
Chest physiotherapy
Encourage deep breathing
Pain management
Monitor for complications

## Slide 19

Massive Hemothorax
Accumulation of >1500 ml of blood in the pleural space or continuous bleeding >200 ml/hr.

## Slide 20

Aetiology
Blunt trauma
Penetrating trauma
Rib fractures injuring vessels

## Slide 21

Pathophysiology
Blood fills pleural cavity
Lung collapse
Reduced venous return
Hypovolemic shock

## Slide 22

Clinical Features
Severe dyspnea
Hypotension
Tachycardia
Dullness to percussion
Decreased breath sounds

## Slide 23

Investigations
Chest X-ray
Ultrasound (FAST)
CT scan
Hb/Hct levels

## Slide 24

Complications
Shock
Respiratory failure
Death

## Slide 25

Management
Fluid resuscitation
Blood transfusion
Chest tube insertion
Emergency thoracotomy if ongoing bleeding

## Slide 26

Nursing Management
Pre-operative
ABC assessment
Oxygen therapy
Large-bore IV access
Prepare blood transfusion
Post-operative
Monitor chest drainage
Frequent vital signs
Maintain chest tube
Prevent hypothermia

## Slide 27

Summary
Thoracic trauma is a major cause of morbidity and mortality
Tension pneumothorax is a clinical emergency requiring immediate decompression
Flail chest leads to ineffective ventilation and often requires respiratory support
Massive hemothorax causes both respiratory compromise and hypovolemic shock
Early recognition and prompt management are critical to survival
