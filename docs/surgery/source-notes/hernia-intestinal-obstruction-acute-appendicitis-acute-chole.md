## Slide 2

Hernia: Definition
A hernia is the protrusion of an organ, part of an organ, or tissue through an abnormal opening or weakness in the wall of the cavity that normally contains it.
Commonly, abdominal contents protrude through weakened areas of the abdominal wall.

## Slide 3

Hernia: Anatomy
A hernia consists of:
• Hernial orifice – the defect/opening
• Hernial sac – peritoneal pouch
• Contents – bowel, omentum, bladder etc.
• Coverings – surrounding tissue layers

## Slide 5

Hernia: Aetiology / Risk Factors
Congenital causes:
Patent processus vaginalis
Weak abdominal musculature
Acquired causes:
• Aging • Obesity • Pregnancy • Chronic cough
• Chronic constipation • Heavy lifting • Ascites
• Previous surgery • Trauma • Malnutrition

## Slide 6

Types of Hernia (1)
1. Inguinal Hernia
(a) Indirect: passes through deep inguinal ring, may descend into scrotum, usually congenital.
(b) Direct: protrudes through weak posterior wall, usually acquired, common in older men.
2. Femoral Hernia
Through femoral canal
More common in females
High risk of strangulation

## Slide 7

Types of Hernia (2)
3. Umbilical Hernia
Occurs at umbilicus
Common in infants, obese adults, pregnancy
4. Incisional Hernia
Through previous surgical scar
Due to poor wound healing
5. Epigastric Hernia
Through linea alba above umbilicus
6. Hiatus Hernia
Part of stomach protrudes through diaphragm
7. Obturator Hernia
• Through obturator foramen

## Slide 8

Clinical Classification of Hernia
• Reducible – can be pushed back
• Irreducible/Incarcerated – cannot be reduced
• Obstructed – bowel lumen obstructed
• Strangulated – blood supply compromised (emergency)

## Slide 9

Hernia: Pathophysiology
Weakness in abdominal wall + increased intra-abdominal pressure
→ protrusion of abdominal contents
→ venous congestion
→ edema
→ obstruction
→ ischemia
→ necrosis (if strangulated)

## Slide 10

Hernia: Signs, Investigations & Surgical Management
Signs/Symptoms:
• Swelling • Groin discomfort • Dragging sensation
• Pain on coughing • Cough impulse • Reducibility
Strangulated hernia: severe pain, tenderness, vomiting, abdominal distension, fever
Investigations:
• Clinical examination • Ultrasound • CT scan • CBC • Electrolytes
Surgical Management:
• Herniorrhaphy
• Hernioplasty (mesh repair)
• Lichtenstein repair
• Laparoscopic repair

## Slide 11

Hernia: Nursing Management
Pre-op:
• Assessment (pain, bowel function, vital signs)
• NPO • IV fluids • Consent • Baseline labs • Skin prep
• Patient education • Deep breathing exercises
Post-op:
• Monitor vital signs, pain, wound
• Analgesics • Early ambulation • Wound care
• Prevent constipation
• Avoid heavy lifting • Follow-up

## Slide 12

Intestinal Obstruction
Definition:
Partial or complete blockage of intestinal contents.
Aetiology:
Mechanical – adhesions, hernia, tumors, volvulus, intussusception
Functional – paralytic ileus, electrolyte imbalance
Pathophysiology:
Obstruction → fluid accumulation → bowel distension
→ decreased blood flow → ischemia → necrosis/perforation

## Slide 13

Intestinal Obstruction: Signs, Investigations, Management
Signs/Symptoms:
• Colicky pain • Vomiting • Constipation
• Distension • Failure to pass flatus
Investigations:
• CBC • Electrolytes • Abdominal X-ray • CT scan
Surgical Management:
• Laparotomy • Adhesiolysis • Bowel resection
• Hernia repair • Colostomy (if needed)
Nursing Management:
Pre-op: NPO, NG tube, IV fluids, electrolyte correction, monitor urine output
Post-op: bowel sounds, NG care, fluid balance, ambulation, wound care

## Slide 14

Acute Appendicitis
Definition:
Acute inflammation of the vermiform appendix.
Aetiology/Risk Factors:
• Fecalith • Lymphoid hyperplasia • Foreign body • Tumor
Pathophysiology:
Luminal obstruction → mucus accumulation
→ bacterial growth → inflammation
→ ischemia → perforation

## Slide 15

Acute Appendicitis: Clinical Features & Management
Signs/Symptoms:
• Periumbilical pain → right iliac fossa
• Anorexia • Nausea • Vomiting • Fever • Rebound tenderness
Investigations:
• CBC • CRP • Ultrasound • CT scan
Surgical Management:
• Appendectomy (open or laparoscopic)
Nursing Management:
Pre-op: NPO, IV fluids, antibiotics, pain assessment
Avoid laxatives and heat application
Post-op: monitor vitals, pain, wound, ambulation, infection signs

## Slide 16

Acute Cholecystitis
Definition:
Acute inflammation of the gallbladder, usually due to gallstone obstruction.
Risk Factors (4 Fs):
• Female • Fat • Forty • Fertile
Others:
• Obesity • Diabetes • Pregnancy • Rapid weight loss
Pathophysiology:
Gallstone obstructs cystic duct
→ bile stasis → inflammation → infection → edema

## Slide 18

Acute Cholecystitis: Clinical Features & Management
Signs/Symptoms:
• Right upper quadrant pain • Fever
• Nausea • Vomiting • Murphy’s sign • Jaundice (sometimes)
Investigations:
• CBC • LFTs • Serum bilirubin • Ultrasound
Surgical Management:
• Laparoscopic cholecystectomy (gold standard)
• Open cholecystectomy
Nursing Management:
Pre-op: NPO, IV fluids, antibiotics, pain control
Post-op: monitor vitals, pain, bile leak, ambulation, low-fat diet education
