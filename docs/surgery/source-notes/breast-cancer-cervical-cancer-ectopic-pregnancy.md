## Slide 2

Breast Cancer
Definition
Breast Cancer is a malignant proliferation of epithelial cells in the breast, most commonly arising from the ducts or lobules.
Aetiology / Risk Factors
Female sex (most common)
Increasing age (>40 years)
Family history (BRCA1, BRCA2 mutations)
Early menarche, late menopause
Nulliparity or late first pregnancy
Obesity (postmenopausal)
Alcohol intake
Previous breast cancer or benign proliferative breast disease

## Slide 3

Pathophysiology & Clinical Features
Pathophysiology
Genetic mutations (e.g., BRCA genes) → uncontrolled cell growth
Malignant transformation begins in ductal/lobular epithelium
Tumor invades surrounding breast tissue
Lymphatic spread → axillary lymph nodes
Hematogenous spread → bone, liver, lungs, brain
Clinical Features
Painless hard breast lump (irregular, fixed)
Skin dimpling
Peau d’orange
Nipple retraction or discharge (may be bloody)
Breast asymmetry
Axillary lymphadenopathy
Ulceration in advanced disease

## Slide 5

Investigations and Surgical Management
Investigations
Mammography (first-line screening tool)
Breast ultrasound
Core needle biopsy (definitive diagnosis)
Fine needle aspiration (FNA)
MRI breast (high-risk patients)
Hormone receptor testing (ER/PR, HER2)
Surgical Management
Lumpectomy (breast-conserving surgery)
Mastectomy (simple or modified radical)
Sentinel lymph node biopsy
Axillary lymph node dissection
Reconstructive breast surgery (optional)

## Slide 7

Cervical Cancer
Definition
Cervical Cancer is a malignant growth of cervical epithelial cells, most commonly caused by persistent infection with high-risk HPV types.
Aetiology / Risk Factors
Human papillomavirus (HPV 16, 18) infection
Early sexual activity
Multiple sexual partners
High parity
Smoking
Immunosuppression (HIV)
Long-term oral contraceptive use
Poor genital hygiene

## Slide 8

Pathophysiology & Clinical Features
Pathophysiology
HPV infects squamous epithelial cells of cervix
Viral DNA integrates into host cells
Dysplasia → Cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN) → carcinoma
Local invasion to vagina, uterus, bladder, rectum
Metastasis via lymphatics
Clinical Features
Postcoital bleeding (early sign)
Foul-smelling vaginal discharge
Irregular vaginal bleeding
Pelvic pain
Dyspareunia
Weight loss (late stage)

## Slide 9

Investigations & Surgical Management
Investigations
Pap smear (screening)
HPV DNA testing
Colposcopy
Cervical biopsy (confirmatory)
Pelvic ultrasound / MRI (staging)
Surgical Management
Cone biopsy (early stage)
Hysterectomy (simple or radical)
Trachelectomy (fertility-sparing in early disease)
Pelvic lymph node dissection
Advanced cases: palliative surgery

Catheter care (urinary retention risk post hysterectomy)
Wound care

## Slide 11

Ectopic Pregnancy
Definition
Ectopic Pregnancy is the implantation of a fertilized ovum outside the uterine cavity, most commonly in the fallopian tube.
Aetiology / Risk Factors
Previous ectopic pregnancy
Pelvic inflammatory disease (PID)
Tubal surgery or scarring
Intrauterine device (IUD) use
Infertility treatments (IVF)
Smoking
Endometriosis

## Slide 12

Pathophysiology & Clinical Features
Pathophysiology
Fertilized ovum implants in fallopian tube
Tubal wall cannot accommodate growing embryo
Tubal distension → rupture
Internal hemorrhage → hypovolemic shock
Clinical Features
Missed period
Lower abdominal pain (unilateral)
Vaginal bleeding (dark spotting)
Shoulder tip pain (internal bleeding irritation)
Dizziness/syncope (rupture)
Signs of shock (tachycardia, hypotension)

## Slide 13

Investigations and Surgical Management
Investigations
Serum β-hCG (abnormal rise)
Transvaginal ultrasound (no intrauterine gestational sac)
Culdocentesis (blood in pouch of Douglas)
Full blood count (anemia)
Laparoscopy (diagnostic gold standard)
Surgical Management
Laparoscopic salpingostomy
Salpingectomy (removal of affected tube)
Emergency laparotomy (ruptured ectopic with shock)

## Slide 14

Nursing Management
Pre-operative
Stabilize patient (IV fluids, blood transfusion)
Monitor vital signs closely
Pain management
Emotional support (pregnancy loss)
Post-operative
Monitor bleeding and shock signs
Fluid balance charting
Wound care
Monitor hCG levels until negative
Family planning counseling

## Slide 15

Imperforate Hymen
Definition
Imperforate Hymen (often clinically associated condition) refers to a congenital obstruction of the vaginal opening due to failure of canalization of the vaginal plate.
Aetiology / Risk Factors
Congenital developmental defect
Failure of Müllerian duct canalization
Genetic predisposition (rare)
No known environmental cause

## Slide 16

Pathophysiology & Clinical Features
Pathophysiology
Vaginal canal fails to open properly
Accumulation of menstrual blood (hematocolpos)
Distension of vagina and uterus
Back pressure → pelvic pain and urinary symptoms
Clinical Features
Primary amenorrhea
Cyclic lower abdominal pain
Bulging bluish hymenal membrane
Urinary retention or constipation
Pelvic mass

## Slide 17

Investigations & Surgical Management
Investigations
Pelvic examination
Ultrasound (shows hematocolpos/hematometra)
MRI pelvis (detailed anatomy)
Hormonal profile (normal reproductive hormones)
Surgical Management
Hymenectomy (cruciate incision)
Vaginal reconstruction if severe
Drainage of accumulated blood

## Slide 19

Bartholin’s Cyst
Definition
Bartholin's Cyst is a fluid-filled swelling that occurs when the duct of the Bartholin gland becomes blocked, causing mucus to accumulate.
The Bartholin glands are located on both sides of the vaginal opening and help lubricate the vagina.
Aetiology / Risk Factors
Infection of the gland
Poor perineal hygiene
Trauma to vulva
Sexually transmitted infections (e.g., gonorrhea, chlamydia)
Common in women of reproductive age

## Slide 20

Pathophysiology & Clinical Features
Pathophysiology
Duct becomes obstructed
Secretions accumulate inside gland
Swelling develops → cyst formation
If infected → abscess forms with pus collection
Clinical Features
Bartholin’s Cyst
Painless vulval swelling
Discomfort while walking or sitting
Dyspareunia (pain during intercourse)
Bartholin’s Abscess
Severe pain
Redness and tenderness
Fever
Difficulty walking or sitting
Pus discharge

## Slide 21

Investigations & Surgical Management
Investigations
Physical examination
Swab for culture and sensitivity if discharge present
STI screening
Biopsy in women >40 years to rule out malignancy
Surgical Management
Incision and drainage (for abscess)
Word catheter insertion
Marsupialization (creating permanent opening)
Excision of gland (recurrent cases)
