## Slide 3

Definition
Lung cancer is a malignant neoplastic disease arising from the epithelial cells of the bronchi, bronchioles, or alveoli of the lungs, characterized by uncontrolled cell growth, invasion of surrounding tissues, and the ability to metastasize to distant organs.
Its broadly classified into two major groups:
Non Small Cell Lung Cancer
Small Cell Lung Cancer

Cigarette Smoking-the most important risk factor

## Slide 12

Surgical Management
Lobectomy
Removal of the entire lobe of the lung containing the tumor.
This is the most commonly performed surgical procedure.
Pneumonectomy
Removal of the entire lung when the tumor is extensive.
Segmentectomy or Wedge Resection
Removal of a small portion of lung tissue when tumors are small or when patients cannot tolerate major surgery.
Lymph Node Dissection
Removal of regional lymph nodes to determine the extent of cancer spread.

## Slide 16

Summary
Lung cancer is a malignant tumor of lung tissue commonly associated with cigarette smoking.
It develops due to genetic mutations caused by carcinogenic exposure, leading to uncontrolled cell growth.
The disease may cause airway obstruction, tissue destruction, and metastasis to distant organs.
Common symptoms include persistent cough, hemoptysis, chest pain, dyspnea, and weight loss.
Diagnosis is confirmed through imaging studies and tissue biopsy.
Surgical resection remains the primary treatment for early-stage lung cancer, particularly non-small cell lung cancer.
Effective perioperative nursing care is essential to prevent respiratory complications and promote recovery after lung surgery.
