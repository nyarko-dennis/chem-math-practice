## Slide 2

Nephrolithiasis
Definition:
Formation/presence of stones in kidneys or urinary tract.
Risk Factors:
• Dehydration
• Low fluid intake
• Hyperparathyroidism
• UTI
• Obesity
• Family history
• Gout
• Urinary stasis

## Slide 3

Nephrolithiasis: Pathophysiology & Clinical Features
Pathophysiology:
Supersaturation → crystal formation → stones → obstruction → hydronephrosis.
Clinical Features:
• Severe flank pain
• Pain radiating to groin
• Hematuria
• Dysuria
• Nausea/vomiting
• Fever

## Slide 5

Nephrolithiasis: Investigations & Management
Investigations:
• Urinalysis
• Urine culture
• Urea/creatinine
• CT scan (gold standard)
• Ultrasound
Surgical Management:
• ESWL- Extracorporeal shockwave lithotripsy
• Ureteroscopy
• Percutaneous nephrolithotomy
Nursing:
Pain control, monitor urine, hydration, infection prevention.

## Slide 6

Testicular Torsion
Definition:
Twisting of spermatic cord causing interrupted blood supply.
Risk Factors:
Bell-clapper deformity
Trauma
Exercise
Adolescence
Previous torsion

## Slide 7

Spermatic Cord

## Slide 8

Testicular Torsion: Pathophysiology & Clinical Features
Pathophysiology:
Twisting → venous obstruction → edema → arterial obstruction → ischemia.
Clinical Features:
Sudden severe scrotal pain
Swollen testis
Nausea/vomiting
High-riding testis
Absent cremasteric reflex

## Slide 9

Testicular Torsion: Investigations & Management
Investigations:
• Clinical diagnosis
• Doppler ultrasound
Surgical Management:
• Emergency detorsion
• Orchidopexy
• Orchidectomy if non-viable
Nursing:
NPO, pain management, emotional support, wound care.

## Slide 10

Hydrocele
Definition:
Fluid accumulation around the testis within tunica vaginalis.
Types:
• Congenital
• Acquired
Risk Factors:
• Trauma
• Infection
• Tumors

## Slide 11

Hydrocele: Clinical Features & Management
Clinical Features:
Painless scrotal swelling
Heaviness
Enlarged scrotum
Positive transillumination
Investigations:
Examination
Ultrasound
Surgical Management:
Hydrocelectomy
Aspiration
Nursing:
Pain control, wound care, scrotal support.

## Slide 13

Cryptorchidism
Definition:
Failure of one or both testes to descend into the scrotum.
Types:
Unilateral
Bilateral

## Slide 14

Cryptorchidism: Risk Factors & Pathophysiology
Risk Factors:
Prematurity
Hormonal abnormalities
Genetic disorders
Low birth weight
Pathophysiology:
Failure of descent → impaired spermatogenesis.

## Slide 15

Cryptorchidism: Clinical Features & Complications
Clinical Features:
Empty scrotum
Non-palpable testis
Inguinal swelling
Complications:
Infertility
Testicular cancer
Torsion
Inguinal hernia

## Slide 16

Cryptorchidism: Investigations & Management
Investigations:
Clinical exam
Ultrasound
MRI
Hormonal studies
Surgical Management:
Orchidopexy before age 1–2 years
Nursing:
Parent education, wound care, follow-up.
