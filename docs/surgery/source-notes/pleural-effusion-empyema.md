## Slide 3

Pleura??
The pleura is a thin, double-layered serous membrane that surrounds the lungs and lines the chest cavity.
It has two layers:
Visceral Pleura
Covers the surface of the lungs directly
Cannot be separated from the lung tissue
Parietal Pleura
Lines the inner surface of the chest wall, diaphragm, and mediastinum

## Slide 5

Pleural Space
The space between the two layers is called the pleural space. It contains a small amount of lubricating pleural fluid.
Functions of the Pleura
Reduces friction
Pleural fluid allows smooth movement during breathing
Facilitates lung expansion
Maintains negative pressure → keeps lungs inflated
Protects the lungs
Acts as a barrier against infection and injury

## Slide 6

Clinical Relevance
Pleural effusion → fluid accumulation
Empyema thoracis → pus in pleural space
Pneumothorax → air in pleural space

## Slide 7

Empyema Thoracis - Definition
Accumulation of pus in pleural space
Usually due to infection (e.g., pneumonia)

## Slide 8

Empyema - Aetiology/Risk Factors
Complicated pneumonia
Tuberculosis
Chest trauma
Thoracic surgery
Lung abscess rupture
Immunosuppression
Aspiration

## Slide 9

Empyema - Pathophysiology (Stages)
Exudative stage: sterile fluid accumulation into the pleural space
Fibrinopurulent stage: bacterial invasion, pus formation. Fibrin deposits cause septation called (loculations)
Organizing stage: thick fibrous peel forms over lung becomes trapped, causing reduced expansion.

## Slide 11

Empyema - Signs & Symptoms
Fever
Pleuritic chest pain
Dyspnea
Productive cough
Reduced chest expansion
Dullness to percussion
Decreased breath sounds
Malaise and weight loss

## Slide 12

Empyema - Investigations
Chest X-ray: pleural opacity
Ultrasound: loculations
CT scan: detailed view
Thoracentesis: pus, low pH/glucose
Pleural fluid culture
Full blood count: ↑WBC

## Slide 13

Empyema - Complications
Sepsis
Bronchopleural fistula
Lung fibrosis (trapped lung)
Respiratory failure
Spread of infection

## Slide 14

Empyema - Surgical Management
Chest tube drainage- breaks loculations and drain pus
Video-Assisted Thoracoscopic Surgery (VATS)
Decortication- removal of fibrous peel
Open thoracotomy (severe cases)

## Slide 15

Chest Tube Care
Keep drainage bottle below chest level (prevents backflow)
Ensure tubing is: Not kinked, Not clamped (unless ordered)
Check for tidaling (normal fluctuation with breathing)
Observe for air leaks (bubbling in water seal chamber

## Slide 16

Chest Tube Care Contd
Record:
Amount
Color
Consistency
Examples:
Serous → normal recovery
Bloody → post-op/trauma
Pus → infection (empyema)

## Slide 17

Chest Tube Care Contd
Keep insertion site clean and dry
Use aseptic technique during dressing changes
Watch for: Redness, Swelling, Discharge.
Criteria for Removal
Drainage is minimal. Usually <50–100 mL/day (some use <150 mL/day depending on protocol)
No air leak. No bubbling in water seal chamber
Lung fully expanded. Confirmed by chest X-ray
Patient is clinically stable. No respiratory distress

## Slide 18

Empyema - Nursing Care (Pre-op)
Monitor vital signs
Administer antibiotics
Oxygen therapy
Prepare for investigations
Patient education
Maintain hydration and nutrition

## Slide 19

Empyema - Nursing Care (Post-op)
Monitor chest tube drainage
Ensure tube patency
Pain management
Deep breathing & spirometry
Semi-Fowler’s position
Monitor for complications
Aseptic technique
Encourage ambulation

## Slide 20

Recurrent Pleural Effusion - Definition
Repeated accumulation of fluid in pleural space
Occurs despite treatment or drainage

## Slide 21

Recurrent Effusion - Aetiology/Risk Factors
Malignancy
Congestive heart failure
Liver cirrhosis
Renal failure
Tuberculosis
Pulmonary embolism

## Slide 22

Recurrent Effusion - Pathophysiology
Imbalance between fluid production & absorption
Increased hydrostatic pressure
Decreased oncotic pressure
Increased permeability
Lymphatic obstruction

## Slide 23

Recurrent Effusion - Signs & Symptoms
Progressive dyspnea
Chest heaviness
Dry cough
Reduced breath sounds
Stony dull percussion
Fatigue

## Slide 24

Recurrent Effusion - Investigations
Chest X-ray- fluid level
Ultrasound- guides drainage
CT scan
Thoracentesis
Pleural biopsy
Blood tests

## Slide 25

Recurrent Effusion - Complications
Lung collapse
Infection (empyema)
Pleural thickening
Respiratory distress

## Slide 26

Recurrent Effusion - Surgical Management
Repeated thoracentesis- temporary relief
Chest tube drainage
Pleurodesis
Indwelling pleural catheter
Pleurectomy- severe cases
