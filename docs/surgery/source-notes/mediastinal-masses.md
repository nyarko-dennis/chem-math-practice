## Slide 3

Thymoma
Thymoma is a tumor arising from the epithelial cells of the thymus gland, located in the anterior mediastinum.
It is the most common tumor of the anterior mediastinum in adults and is frequently associated with autoimmune diseases, especially myasthenia gravis.
SAK-MM

## Slide 4

Aetiology/Risk Factors
The exact cause of thymoma is not fully understood, but several factors are associated with its development:
Autoimmune Disorders
Genetic and Cellular Abnormalities
SAK-MM

## Slide 5

Pathophysiology
The thymus normally functions in T-lymphocyte maturation and immune regulation.
In thymoma:
Abnormal Proliferation of Thymic Epithelial Cells
Local Compression of Mediastinal Structures
Immune System Dysreglation

Mediastinal Lymphoma
Mediastinal lymphoma is a malignant tumor arising from lymphatic tissue within the mediastinum.
It commonly occurs in the anterior or middle mediastinum and may be part of systemic lymphoma.
Two main types include:
Hodgkin lymphoma
Non-Hodgkin lymphoma.

## Slide 20

Germ Cell Tumor
Mediastinal germ cell tumors are tumors arising from primitive germ cells located in the mediastinum, most commonly in the anterior mediastinum.
They may be:
Benign
Teratomas.
Malignant
Seminomas
Non-seminomatous germ cell tumors.
SAK-MM

## Slide 21

Aetiology/Risk Factors
The tumors develop due to abnormal migration of germ cells during embryonic development.
Normally, germ cells migrate to the gonads during fetal life.
When some remain in the mediastinum, they may later develop into tumors.

## Slide 28

Summary
Mediastinal tumors include thymoma, lymphoma, and germ cell tumors.
These tumors most commonly occur in the anterior mediastinum.
They may cause symptoms through compression of vital mediastinal structures such as the trachea, esophagus, and major blood vessels.
Diagnosis relies on imaging studies and biopsy.
Surgical removal is the primary treatment for thymoma and many germ cell tumors, while lymphoma is primarily treated with chemotherapy and radiotherapy.
Nursing care focuses on respiratory monitoring, perioperative support, complication prevention, and patient education.
