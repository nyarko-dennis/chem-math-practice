## Slide 3

Aortic Aneurysm
An Aortic aneurysm is an abnormal localized dilation (widening) of the aorta caused by weakness of the arterial wall.
The aorta normally carries blood from the heart to the body. When the wall becomes weak, the pressure of blood causes the vessel to balloon outward.
An aneurysm is usually diagnosed when the aortic diameter becomes 50% larger than normal.
Types include:
Thoracic aortic aneurysm
Abdominal aortic aneurysm

## Slide 5

Aetiology/Risk Factors
Several conditions weaken the wall of the aorta.
Atherosclerosis
Fatty deposits damage the arterial wall and reduce elasticity.
Hypertension
High blood pressure increases stress on the aortic wall.
Genetic disorders
Examples include:
Marfan syndrome
Ehlers-Danlos syndrome
Trauma
Injury to the chest may damage the aorta.
Infection
Rarely infections can cause mycotic aneurysms.

## Slide 7

Pathophysiology
The aortic wall contains three layers:
Intima (inner layer)
Media (middle muscular layer)
Adventitia (outer layer)
In aneurysm formation:
Degeneration of the media layer occurs.
Elastic fibers break down.
The wall becomes weak.
Continuous blood pressure causes progressive dilation.
As the aneurysm enlarges:
Wall tension increases.
Risk of rupture increases significantly.

Investigations
Ultrasound
Often used for screening abdominal aneurysm.
CT Scan
Most common imaging method.Shows size, shape, and rupture risk.
MRI
Provides detailed vascular imaging.
Aortography
Contrast imaging of the aorta.
Chest X-ray

## Slide 11

Complications
If untreated, aneurysms may lead to:
Rupture (most dangerous complication)
Hemorrhagic shock
Thrombus formation
Embolism
Compression of nearby organs
Organ ischemia
Mortality rate is very high once rupture occurs.

## Slide 12

Surgical Management
Surgery is recommended when aneurysm size is large or symptoms appear.
Open Surgical Repair
The diseased part of the aorta is removed and replaced with a synthetic graft.
Endovascular Aneurysm Repair (EVAR)
A stent graft is inserted through the femoral artery and positioned inside the aneurysm.
Advantages:
Less invasive
Shorter recovery time

## Slide 16

Aortic Dissection Definition
An Aortic dissection occurs when a tear develops in the inner layer of the aorta.
Blood enters the tear and separates the layers of the aortic wall, creating a false channel (false lumen).
This condition is life-threatening and requires emergency treatment.

## Slide 18

Pathophysiology
The process begins with damage to the intima.
Step-by-step process:
Intimal tear occurs.
Blood enters the middle layer (media).
Blood splits the layers apart.
A false lumen forms.
The dissection may extend along the aorta.
This can:
Block blood flow to organs
Cause rupture

## Slide 25

Summary
Aortic aneurysm is a localized dilation of the aorta due to wall weakness.
Aortic dissection is a tear in the aortic wall allowing blood to separate its layers.
Major risk factors include hypertension, atherosclerosis, and connective tissue disorders.
Both conditions may lead to life-threatening rupture.
Diagnosis relies on advanced imaging techniques.
Management includes surgical repair or endovascular procedures.
Nurses play a critical role in early detection, monitoring, and postoperative care.
