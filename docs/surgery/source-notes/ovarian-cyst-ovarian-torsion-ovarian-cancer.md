## Slide 2

Ovarian Cyst
Definition
Ovarian Cyst is a fluid-filled sac that develops within or on the ovary.
Aetiology / Risk Factors
Hormonal imbalance
Failure of follicle rupture during ovulation
Endometriosis
Pregnancy
Polycystic ovarian syndrome (PCOS), Now PMOS
Previous ovarian cyst

## Slide 3

Pathophysiology & Clinical Features
Pathophysiology
Follicle or ovarian tissue fills with fluid
Cyst enlarges over time
Large cysts may rupture, bleed, or twist
Clinical Features
Lower abdominal/pelvic pain
Abdominal bloating
Irregular menstruation
Dyspareunia
Pressure symptoms (frequency, constipation)
Some are asymptomatic

## Slide 4

Investigations & Surgical Management
Investigations
Pelvic ultrasound (main investigation)
Pregnancy test
CA-125 (if malignancy suspected)
CT/MRI scan
Surgical Management
Cystectomy (removal of cyst)
Oophorectomy (removal of ovary)
Laparoscopy or laparotomy

## Slide 6

Ovarian Torsion
Definition
Ovarian Torsion is the twisting of the ovary around its supporting ligaments, leading to reduced blood flow.
Aetiology / Risk Factors
Ovarian cyst or mass
Enlarged ovary
Pregnancy
Fertility treatment
Previous torsion

## Slide 7

Pathophysiology & Clinical Features
Pathophysiology
Ovary twists around vascular pedicle
Venous obstruction occurs first
Arterial supply becomes compromised
Ischemia and necrosis may develop
Clinical Features
Sudden severe unilateral pelvic pain
Nausea and vomiting
Pelvic tenderness
Fever (late sign)
Adnexal mass

## Slide 8

Investigations and Surgical Management
Investigations
Pelvic ultrasound with Doppler
Pregnancy test
CBC
Laparoscopy (confirmatory)
Surgical Management
Detorsion (untwisting ovary)
Cyst removal
Oophorectomy if ovary necrotic

## Slide 10

Ovarian Cancer
Definition
Ovarian Cancer is a malignant tumor arising from ovarian tissue.
Aetiology / Risk Factors
Increasing age
Family history (BRCA1/BRCA2)
Nulliparity
Early menarche/late menopause
Endometriosis
Obesity

## Slide 11

Pathophysiology & Clinical Features
Pathophysiology
Malignant transformation of ovarian cells
Tumor spreads within pelvis and abdomen
Peritoneal dissemination and ascites occur
Metastasis may develop
Signs and Symptoms
Abdominal bloating/distension
Pelvic pain
Early satiety
Weight loss
Ascites
Irregular menstruation

## Slide 12

Investigations & Surgical Management
Investigations
Pelvic ultrasound
CA-125 blood test
CT/MRI scan
Biopsy/histology
Surgical Management
Total abdominal hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy
Tumor debulking surgery
Omentectomy

## Slide 14

Endometrial Cancer
Definition
Endometrial Cancer is a malignant growth arising from the endometrial lining of the uterus.
Aetiology / Risk Factors
Prolonged estrogen exposure
Obesity
Diabetes mellitus
Hypertension
Nulliparity
Late menopause
Tamoxifen use

## Slide 15

Pathophysiology & Clinical Features
Pathophysiology
Excess estrogen stimulates endometrial hyperplasia
Hyperplasia progresses to malignancy
Tumor invades myometrium and surrounding structures
Clinical Features
Postmenopausal bleeding
Abnormal uterine bleeding
Pelvic pain
Vaginal discharge
Weight loss (late stage)

## Slide 16

Investigations & Surgical Management
Investigations
Transvaginal ultrasound
Endometrial biopsy (gold standard)
Hysteroscopy
CT/MRI for staging
Surgical Management
Total hysterectomy
Bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy
Lymph node dissection
