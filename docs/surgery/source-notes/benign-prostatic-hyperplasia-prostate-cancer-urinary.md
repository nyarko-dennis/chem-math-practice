## Slide 2

Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia(BPH)
Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia is a non-malignant enlargement of the prostate gland causing urethral compression and urinary obstruction.

## Slide 3

Aetiology/Risk Factors & Pathophysiology
Aetiology/Risk Factors
Ageing (most important)
Androgen influence (DHT)
Family history
Obesity
Sedentary lifestyle
Metabolic syndrome
Pathophysiology
Prostate gland enlargement (periurethral zone) →Compression of prostatic urethra →Bladder outlet obstruction →Increased bladder pressure →Detrusor muscle hypertrophy →Eventually bladder decompensation.

## Slide 4

Clinical Features
Obstructive:
Weak urinary stream
Hesitancy
Straining
Dribbling
Incomplete emptying
Irritative:
Frequency
Nocturia
Urgency

## Slide 7

Prostate Cancer
Definition
Prostate cancer is a malignant tumour of the prostate gland, most commonly adenocarcinoma.
Risk Factors
Age >50 years
Family history
African ancestry
High-fat diet
Obesity
Androgen exposure

## Slide 8

Pathophysiology & Clinical Features
Pathophysiology
Normal epithelium →Genetic mutation →Dysplasia →Carcinoma in situ →Invasive adenocarcinoma →Metastasis (bone most common)
Clinical Features
Early: often asymptomatic
Urinary obstruction symptoms
Hematuria
Bone pain (metastasis)
Weight loss
Back pain

## Slide 9

Investigations & Surgical Management
Investigations
PSA (elevated)

Prostate biopsy (confirmatory)
MRI pelvis
Bone scan (metastasis)
Surgical Management
Radical prostatectomy
TURP (palliative)
Radiotherapy
Hormonal therapy (androgen deprivation)

## Slide 11

Urinary Retention
Definition
Urinary retention is the inability to completely empty the bladder.
Types
Acute urinary retention
Chronic urinary retention
Aetiology / Risk Factors
BPH (most common in males)
Urethral stricture
Neurological disorders
Drugs (anticholinergics, opioids)
Post-surgery
Infection

## Slide 12

Pathophysiology & Clinical Features
Pathophysiology
Bladder outlet obstruction or detrusor failure →Urine accumulation →Bladder distension →Increased intravesical pressure →Pain and possible kidney damage
Clinical Features
Inability to pass urine
Suprapubic pain
Distended bladder
Overflow incontinence (chronic)
Restlessness

## Slide 13

Investigations & Management
Investigations

Urinalysis
Renal function tests
Ultrasound
Management
Emergency Catheterization (Foley catheter)
Suprapubic catheter if needed

## Slide 14

Bladder Cancer
Definition
Bladder cancer is a malignancy arising from the bladder epithelium (usually transitional cell carcinoma).
Risk Factors
Smoking (strongest risk factor)
Occupational exposure (dyes, rubber industry)
Chronic cystitis
Schistosoma haematobium infection
Age >55 years
Pathophysiology
Chronic irritation/carcinogen exposure →Urothelial mutation →Dysplasia →Carcinoma in situ →Invasive tumor → muscle invasion → metastasis

## Slide 17

Varicocele
Definition
Varicocele is abnormal dilatation of the pampiniform venous plexus within the scrotum.
Aetiology / Risk Factors
Incompetent venous valves
Increased venous pressure
Left-sided (most common due to drainage into renal vein)
Nutcracker syndrome
Heavy lifting

## Slide 18

Pathophysiology & Clinical Features
Pathophysiology
Valve incompetence →Venous reflux →Pampiniform plexus dilation →Increased testicular temperature →Impaired spermatogenesis
Clinical Features
“Bag of worms” scrotal mass
Dull aching scrotal pain
Infertility
Testicular atrophy (severe cases)
