## Slide 2

Obstructive Jaundice
Obstructive jaundice refers to yellow discoloration of the skin, sclera, and mucous membranes caused by obstruction to the normal flow of bile from the liver to the duodenum, resulting in accumulation of conjugated bilirubin in the bloodstream.
It is primarily a surgical condition because many causes require decompression or removal of obstruction.

## Slide 7

Pathophysiology
Normal bile flow:
Liver↓Hepatic ducts↓Common bile duct↓Duodenum
When obstruction occurs:
Biliary obstruction↓Bile cannot reach intestine↓Conjugated bilirubin refluxes into blood↓Hyperbilirubinemia↓Jaundice
Because bile does not reach the intestine:
Reduced fat digestion↓Steatorrhea
Reduced bilirubin in stool↓Clay-colored stool
Excess bilirubin excreted in urine↓Dark urine
Prolonged obstruction may lead to:
Liver dysfunction
Infection
Sepsis

## Slide 12

Acute Pancreatitis
Definition
Acute pancreatitis is sudden inflammation of the pancreas caused by premature activation of pancreatic digestive enzymes, leading to autodigestion of pancreatic tissue.
It may be mild or life-threatening.

## Slide 13

Aetiology/Risk Factors
Gallstones
Most common.
Alcohol abuse
Trauma
Steroids
Metabolic causes
Examples:
Hypercalcemia
Hypertriglyceridemia
Autoimmune disease
Scorpion sting (rare)
Hereditary causes
ERCP complications

Severe cases may cause:

Pancreatic Cancer
Definition
A malignant tumor arising from pancreatic tissue, commonly from the pancreatic ductal epithelium.
Most are adenocarcinomas.
Very aggressive with poor prognosis.

## Slide 24

Anal Fissure( Definition & Risk Factors/Aetiology)
An anal fissure is a small longitudinal tear in the mucosa of the anal canal, usually located in the posterior midline.
It causes severe pain during defecation.
Aetiology/Risk Factors
Chronic constipation
Passage of hard stool
Repeated straining
Childbirth
Chronic diarrhea
Trauma

## Slide 25

Pathophysiology
Hard stool/trauma↓Anal mucosal tear↓Pain↓Internal sphincter spasm↓Reduced blood flow↓Delayed healing
