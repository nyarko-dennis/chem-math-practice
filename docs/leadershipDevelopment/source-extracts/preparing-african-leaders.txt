                          PREPARING AFRICAN LEADERS
In the ancient times, Africa used to have a system of training leaders. These systems were
built over many years and based on the African culture and tradition. But today that system
has been replaced with the western world’s system.

This chapter is not focused on comparing western systems to African methods of handling
leadership, but to rather clarify on these two concepts which are: (1.) the factors common to
African companies’ top management approaches, this simply means are they acting like a
primus inter pares or the (rooted in local norms) or more like a coconut (Western style
leadership) and (2.) which is the expectation of the local employees to their superiors.

Before Africa was colonized, Africa was structured into clans and kingdoms where the kings
and chiefs were the rulers. Even with kings and chiefs being the centre of power some parts
of Africa for example in Rwanda certain clans like Basindi,Bega etc could make their own
decisions without always going to the chief’s palace.

Despite being diverse in countries Africans think alike, we believe that life is seen as a
universal current that flows and our goal is to protect it. People also aim to live in harmony
because it is believed that nature and the spiritual world are one.

In all the communities studied, A chiefs first priority is to protect social energy and to
encourage interaction among different communities. Also many Africans are community
minded. There was a man named Kitenge, he started a cheese factory in Dr Congo he was
trained in Paris, but he also used African values instead of just western methods. He did not
ask his rich uncles for money and luckily his business grew. He hired many workers through
friends and family networks. Workers could train a relative to replace them when sick. He
also chose local African workers over Western experts.This mix of styles encouraged
punctuality and made the company successful

To conclude it is not said that the primus inter pares is way better or functional as compared
to the “coconut” style but a leader needs to create balance between the two in decision
making, how authority is expressed and also not to forget how one communicates with the
people.
