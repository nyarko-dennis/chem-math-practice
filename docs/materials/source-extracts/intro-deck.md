SMART AND ADVANCED MATERIALS

<!-- Slide 3 -->
Smart Materials: Historical Background
Emerged mainly in the late 20th century
Developed through advances in materials science, electronics, and engineering
Designed to respond automatically to environmental changes

<!-- Slide 4 -->
Smart Materials: Types
• Shape Memory Alloys (SMAs)
• Piezoelectric materials
• Magnetostrictive materials
• Electrochromic materials
• Smart polymers

<!-- Slide 5 -->
Smart Materials: Composition & Properties
Composition:
• Metals, ceramics, polymers, composites
Properties:
• Self-sensing
• Self-adjusting
• Shape-changing
• Adaptive behavior

<!-- Slide 6 -->
Smart Materials: Production & Applications
Production:
• Alloy manufacturing
• Polymer synthesis
• Thin-film deposition
Applications:
• Aerospace
• Medical devices
• Smart windows
• Robotics

<!-- Slide 7 -->
Smart Materials: Advantages, Limitations & Examples
Advantages:
• Efficient
• Safer
• Reduced maintenance
Limitations:
• Expensive
• Complex manufacturing
Examples:
• Millau Viaduct
• International Space Station

<!-- Slide 8 -->
Advanced Materials: Historical Background
Began in the late 20th century
Driven by demand for stronger, lighter, and more efficient materials

<!-- Slide 9 -->
Advanced Materials: Types
• Nanomaterials
• Advanced composites
• Biomaterials
• Graphene
• High-performance ceramics

<!-- Slide 10 -->
Advanced Materials: Composition & Properties
Composition:
• Engineered at micro/nano scales
• Often combine multiple materials
Properties:
• Lightweight
• Strong
• Durable
• Corrosion resistant

<!-- Slide 11 -->
Advanced Materials: Production & Applications
Production:
• Nanotechnology
• 3D Printing
• Chemical Vapor Deposition
Applications:
• Aircraft
• Biomedical implants
• Electronics
• Renewable energy

<!-- Slide 12 -->
Advanced Materials: Advantages, Limitations & Examples
Advantages:
• High performance
• Energy efficient
Limitations:
• Expensive
• Recycling challenges
Examples:
• Boeing 787 Dreamliner
• Tesla Model S

<!-- Slide 13 -->
Transition, Modern Relevance & Conclusion
Transition:
• Need for better performance led from smart to advanced materials
Modern Relevance:
• Aerospace, medicine, electronics, transport
Conclusion:
• Smart materials respond to changes.
• Advanced materials deliver exceptional performance.
