  Classification of Steel

Focus: AISI/SAE System and Other
     Classification Methods
               Introduction
• • Steel is classified to understand its
  properties and applications.
• • Standard classification helps engineers
  select the right type for the job.
• • Major systems include AISI/SAE,
  composition-based, microstructure-based,
  and usage-based classifications.
   What is AISI/SAE Designation?
• • AISI: American Iron and Steel Institute
• • SAE: Society of Automotive Engineers
• • Uses a 4-digit number to represent steel
  type and carbon content.
• • Commonly used in North America and
  worldwide for steel standardization.
   How the AISI/SAE 4-Digit Code
              Works
• • 1st digit: Steel type or major alloying
  element
• • 2nd digit: Sub-category or secondary
  element (e.g., sulfur, manganese)
• • Last 2 digits: Carbon content in hundredths
  of a percent
• Example: SAE 1045 = Plain carbon steel with
  0.45% carbon
        First Digit – Steel Type
• • 1XXX – Plain Carbon Steel
• • 2XXX – Nickel Steel
• • 3XXX – Nickel-Chromium Steel
• • 4XXX – Molybdenum Steel
• • 5XXX – Chromium Steel
• • 6XXX – Chromium-Vanadium Steel
• • 9XXX – Silicon-Manganese Steel
          Example: SAE 1045
• • 1 = Plain Carbon Steel
• • 0 = No secondary alloying element
• • 45 = 0.45% carbon content
• Properties:
• - Medium strength
• - Used in gears, shafts, machinery
          Example: SAE 4340
• • 4 = Molybdenum Steel
• • 3 = Nickel-Chromium-Molybdenum alloy
• • 40 = 0.40% carbon
• Properties:
• - High strength and toughness
• - Used in aerospace and automotive
        Stainless Steel Examples
• • AISI 304: Austenitic stainless steel (18% Cr,
  8% Ni)
• • AISI 316: Similar to 304 but includes Mo for
  better corrosion resistance
• • AISI 410: Martensitic stainless steel (heat-
  treatable)
 Other Steel Classification Systems
• 1. By Composition:
• - Carbon Steel, Alloy Steel, Stainless Steel

• 2. By Production Method:
• - Bessemer, Electric Arc, Basic Oxygen

• 3. By Microstructure:
• - Ferritic, Austenitic, Martensitic, Bainitic,
  Pearlitic
      Why Classification Matters
• • Helps in choosing the right steel for
  strength, weldability, and corrosion resistance
• • Ensures compatibility in global
  manufacturing
• • Aids in design, safety, and material cost
  management
                  Summary
• • AISI/SAE system uses a 4-digit code to
  classify steel by composition
• • Other classifications include microstructure,
  production method, and use
• • Understanding steel classification is essential
  for engineering and manufacturing
