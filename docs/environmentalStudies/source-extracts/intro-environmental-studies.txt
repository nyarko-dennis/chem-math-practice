What is Environmental Studies?
An interdisciplinary field at the heart of our planet's future

   Environmental Studies is the systematic, interdisciplinary examination
   of the environment and the relationship between humans and the
   natural world.

    Natural Sciences                                                                                 Why it matters
    Biology, chemistry, ecology, geology — understanding how natural systems work.
                                                                                       › Climate change is accelerating

    Social Sciences                                                                    › Biodiversity loss threatens ecosystems
    Economics, political science, anthropology — how societies shape and are shaped
    by the environment.                                                                › Pollution affects human health

    Humanities                                                                         › Resource depletion challenges economies
    History, ethics, philosophy — our values, culture, and moral responsibilities to
    nature.                                                                            › Sustainable solutions are urgently needed

Introduction to Environmental Studies | First Year Lecture

                                           Key Takeaways
Environmental Studies is inherently interdisciplinary — science, policy, ethics, and social dimensions all matter.

Ecosystems provide services essential for human wellbeing; biodiversity loss threatens these services.

Climate change is the most urgent global environmental challenge, requiring both mitigation and adaptation.

Pollution, resource depletion, and habitat destruction are interconnected crises demanding systemic solutions.

The SDGs provide a global framework; local action — like Ghana's sanitation challenges — is equally vital.

Environmental scientists use diverse methods: field work, modelling, GIS, policy analysis, and community research.

Key Takeaways

•   The environment is a shared responsibility of all stakeholders
•   Conservation requires scientific understanding paired with policy action
•   Sustainable development balances economy, ecology & social equity
•   Pollution control demands technology, legislation & public awareness
•   Biodiversity protection is essential for maintaining ecosystem services
•   Individual behaviour change drives collective environmental health
