# Goals and Scope of Palliative Care

## Goals of Palliative Care

- Regard dying as a **normal process** — neither hasten nor postpone death.
- Affirm life.
- Support holistic patient care and enhance quality of life.
- Offer support to patients to **live as actively as possible until death**.

## Scope of Palliative Care

- Offers a support system to help **patients live as actively as possible**.
- Offers a support system to help the **family cope** during the patient's illness and in their own bereavement.
- Uses a **team approach** to address the needs of patients and their families.
- Integrates palliative care into all levels of healthcare to ensure individuals receive holistic support, including pain management, emotional care, and spiritual comfort during their final stages of life.

## Why Quality End-of-Life Care Matters

Death is a natural and inevitable part of the life cycle. Every individual is aware that at some point life will come to an end. While this reality is universal, society's approach to dying has evolved significantly over the past two decades.

In earlier times, death was often viewed with fear, denial, or avoidance. However, there has been growing recognition among healthcare providers, policymakers, and the public of the need to ensure that people not only live well but also **die with dignity and comfort**.

In modern healthcare, there is increasing emphasis on improving the quality of care at the end of life, focusing not just on prolonging life but also on:

- Relieving suffering
- Addressing psychological and spiritual needs
- Supporting families

This shift has been influenced by the development of palliative care and hospice services, which aim to enhance the quality of life for patients with life-limiting illnesses such as cancer, AIDS, organ failure, and advanced neurological disorders.

End-of-life care recognizes that dying is a **personal and meaningful process**, requiring a compassionate, patient-centered approach. The WHO (2023) stresses that palliative care should be integrated into all levels of healthcare.

The increasing burden of chronic and terminal illnesses has made it essential for healthcare systems to prioritize:

- Effective communication
- Ethical decision-making
- Respect for patients' autonomy at the end of life

The goal is to help patients experience a peaceful and dignified death, free from unnecessary suffering, and to provide emotional and psychological support to their loved ones.

## Who Gives Palliative Care?

Any healthcare provider can give palliative care. Some providers specialize in it. Palliative care may be given by:

- A team of doctors
- Nurses
- Registered dietitians
- Social workers
- Psychologists
- Massage therapists
- Chaplains

Palliative care may be offered by **hospitals, outpatient clinics, home care agencies, cancer centers, and long-term care facilities**.
