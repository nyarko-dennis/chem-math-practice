# Hospice Care

## What Is Hospice Care?

**Hospice care** is a form of palliative care given to a person with an **incurable disease**. It exists to provide support and care for persons in the last phases of incurable disease so that they might live as fully and comfortably as possible.

It focuses on **caring, not curing**. When a person has a terminal diagnosis (usually defined as having a **life expectancy of 6 months or less**) and is approaching the end of life, he or she might be eligible to receive hospice care.

Hospice is traditionally limited to the projected last 6 months of life. Hospice is **not a place but a concept of care** that provides compassion, concern, and support for the dying.

Hospice and palliative care are frequently used interchangeably.

## Hospice vs. Palliative Care: Key Difference

The major difference between palliative care and hospice care:

| Feature | Palliative Care | Hospice Care |
|---|---|---|
| Curative treatments | Can be received **simultaneously** with palliative treatments | Provided once a person decides to **forgo** curative treatments |
| Focus | Symptom relief at any stage of serious illness | Comfort care in the last ~6 months of life |
| Location | Hospitals, clinics, home, long-term care | Home, inpatient settings, long-term care facilities |
| Emphasis | Quality of life alongside disease management | Palliative rather than curative care; **quality over quantity** of life |

All hospices emphasize palliative rather than curative care, and quality rather than quantity of life.

## Origin of Hospice

The concept of hospice is derived from **medieval times**, when the term *hospice* was used to identify a particular place where travelers could find rest and comfort. The word *hospice* originates in the **Latin root for hospitality and hospitable** — to provide such comfort, especially to the dying patient.

- Hospices were originally **places of rest for travelers in the 4th century**.
- In the **19th century**, a religious order established hospices for the dying in **Ireland and London**.

## Similarities Between Hospice and Palliative Care

Hospice care and palliative care are very similar when it comes to the most important issues for dying people:

- **Hospice provides palliative care.** Palliative care is both a method of administering "comfort" care and, increasingly, an administered system — most prevalently provided by hospitals.
- As a supplement to some traditional care options, both hospice and palliative care protocols call for patients to receive a **combined approach** where medications, day-to-day care, equipment, bereavement counseling, and symptom treatment are administered through a single program.
