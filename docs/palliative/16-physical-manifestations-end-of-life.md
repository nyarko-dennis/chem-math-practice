# Physical Manifestations at End of Life

As death approaches, predictable physical changes occur in **multiple body systems**. Knowing these changes helps the nurse anticipate needs, educate the family, and provide appropriate comfort care.

## Sensory System

### Hearing
- **Usually the last sense to disappear**
- Continue speaking gently and respectfully to the patient even when they appear unresponsive

### Touch
- Decreased perception of pain and touch
- Decreased sensation overall

### Taste and Smell
- Decreased with disease progression

### Sight
- **Blurring of vision**
- Sinking and glazing of the eyes
- **Blink reflex absent**
- Eyelids remain **half-open**

## Cardiovascular System

- **Increased heart rate** initially; later **slowing and weakening of pulse**
- **Irregular rhythm**
- **Decreased blood pressure**
- **Delayed absorption** of drugs administered intramuscularly or subcutaneously

## Respiratory System

- **Increased respiratory rate**
- **Cheyne–Stokes respiration** — a pattern characterized by alternating periods of **apnea and deep, rapid breathing**
- **Inability to cough or clear secretions**, resulting in grunting, gurgling, or noisy congested breathing — the **"death rattle"**
- Irregular breathing, gradually slowing down to **terminal gasps** (sometimes described as agonal breathing)

## Urinary System

- **Gradual decrease in urine output**
- **Incontinence of urine**
- **Inability to urinate**

## Gastrointestinal System

- **Slowing or cessation of GI function** (may be enhanced by pain-relieving drugs such as opioids)
- **Accumulation of gas**
- **Distention and nausea**
- **Loss of sphincter control**, producing incontinence
- **Bowel movement** before imminent death or at the time of death

## Musculoskeletal System

- **Gradual loss of ability to move**
- **Sagging of the jaw** resulting from loss of facial muscle tone
- **Difficulty speaking**
- **Swallowing becoming more difficult**
- Difficulty maintaining body posture and alignment
- **Loss of gag reflex**
- **Jerking** seen in patients on large amounts of opioids

## Integumentary System

- **Mottling** on hands, feet, arms, and legs
- **Cold, clammy skin**
- **Cyanosis** of nose, nail beds, and knees
- **"Waxlike" skin** when very near death

## Summary Table

| System | Key Manifestations |
|---|---|
| **Sensory** | Hearing last to go; vision blurs; eyes glaze; eyelids half-open |
| **Cardiovascular** | Rate ↑ then ↓; weak/irregular pulse; BP ↓; poor drug absorption (IM/SC) |
| **Respiratory** | Cheyne–Stokes pattern; "death rattle"; terminal gasps |
| **Urinary** | ↓ urine output; incontinence; eventual anuria |
| **Gastrointestinal** | GI slowdown; gas/distention/nausea; sphincter loss |
| **Musculoskeletal** | ↓ movement; sagging jaw; difficulty speaking/swallowing; opioid-related jerking |
| **Integumentary** | Mottling; cold, clammy skin; cyanosis; waxlike skin near death |

## Nursing Implications

- **Continue communication** — assume hearing is intact, even at the very end.
- **Reposition** the patient to ease breathing and protect skin integrity.
- **Manage secretions** with positioning and (if appropriate) suctioning or anticholinergic drugs.
- **Switch to non-IM routes** for medications as absorption declines (oral, sublingual, rectal, subcutaneous).
- **Educate the family** about what to expect — death rattle, Cheyne–Stokes breathing, and mottling can be alarming if unexplained.
- **Provide skin care** — mottled, fragile skin requires gentle handling.
- **Offer warm coverings** as the patient's body temperature drops.
