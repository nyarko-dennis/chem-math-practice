# Cultural Competence in Palliative Care

## Culture of Palliative Care

Nursing care is **not "cookie cutter care" — one size does not fit all**. Perhaps nowhere is this more important than when a nurse provides palliative care.

Palliative care is **very individualized** — comprehensive, holistic nursing care that meets the patient's needs.

Meeting a patient's needs can be challenging, but meeting palliative care needs **in the context of cultural diversity** is even more challenging for those unaccustomed to doing so.

Research has shown that:

- **Cultural knowledge and awareness** in nursing is extremely important.
- **Lack of it may cause deficits in practice**, and nurses may have **ethnocentric and stereotyped attitudes** toward patients.

### The Foundational Theory

**Madeline Leininger** is considered the founder of the theory of **transcultural nursing** (also called the **Culture Care Theory**).

> Culture is **learned by each generation** through both formal and informal experiences.

Patients do not exist in a vacuum — they exist in a complex social system made up of many cultural components. These shape:

- The languages we speak
- The foods we eat
- The customs we practice
- Our faith and belief traditions
- Family values
- Recreational activities, music, art

Embedded in this cultural matrix is **how a person views and reacts to illness**, which exists within a complex cultural belief system.

In patients with chronic illnesses requiring palliative care, behavior is shaped by **culture and faith traditions**. The type of care a patient may be willing to receive and partake in is often dictated by strong cultural and faith beliefs that may not seem easily understood by those outside the culture. This shapes:

- Wanting to **know their diagnosis**
- **Accepting their diagnosis**
- **Seeking** or **refusing care**

## Cultural Competence

**Cultural competence** is the ability to **understand, appreciate, and work with individuals from cultures other than your own**.

It involves:

- **Awareness and acceptance** of cultural differences
- **Self-awareness**
- **Knowledge** of the patient's culture
- **Adaptation of skills** to meet the patient's needs

## The Four Components of Cultural Competence

### 1. Cultural Awareness

**Description:** Ability to understand patients' unique cultural needs.

**How to develop:**
- Identify your own cultural background, values, and beliefs, especially as related to health and healthcare.
- Examine your own **cultural biases** toward people whose cultures differ from your own.

### 2. Cultural Knowledge

**Description:** Process of learning key aspects of a group's culture, especially as it relates to health and healthcare practices. **Patients are the best source of information about their culture.**

**How to develop:**
- Learn basic general information about predominant cultural groups in your geographic area. **Cultural pocket guides** can be a good resource.
- Assess patients for presence or absence of cultural traits based on understanding of generalizations about a cultural group.
- **Do not make assumptions** based on cultural background — the degree of acculturation varies among individuals.
- Read research studies that describe cultural differences.
- Read ethnic newspaper articles and books.
- View documentaries about cultural groups.

### 3. Cultural Skill

**Description:** Ability to collect relevant cultural data — performance of a cultural assessment.

**How to develop:**
- **Be alert** for unexpected responses, especially as related to cultural issues.
- Become aware of cultural differences in predominant ethnic groups.
- Develop assessment skills to do a competent cultural assessment for any patient.
- Learn assessment skills for different cultural groups, including cultural beliefs and practices.

### 4. Cultural Encounter

**Description:** Direct cross-cultural interactions between people from culturally diverse backgrounds; engaged contact with a cultural group to enhance understanding of its values and beliefs.

**How to develop:**
- **Create opportunities to interact** with predominant cultural groups.
- Attend cultural events — religious ceremonies, significant life passage rituals, social events, demonstrations of cultural practices.
- Visit markets and restaurants in ethnic neighborhoods.
- Explore ethnic neighborhoods, listen to different ethnic music, learn games of various ethnic groups.
- Visit or volunteer at **health fairs** in local ethnic neighborhoods.
- Learn about prominent cultural beliefs and practices, and **incorporate this knowledge into nursing care planning**.

## Cultural Factors Affecting Health and Healthcare

### Beliefs and Practices
- Care provided in established healthcare programs may not be perceived as culturally relevant.
- **Religious reasons, beliefs, or practices** may affect a person's decision to seek (or not seek) healthcare.
- Patients may delay seeking healthcare because of **fear** or dependence on **folk medicine and herbal remedies**.
- Patients may **stop treatment or discontinue visits** because symptoms are no longer present and they believe further care is not required.
- Some patients **associate hospitals and extended care facilities with death**.

### Economic Factors
- Patients may not get healthcare because they **cannot pay for it** or because of **costs associated with travel** for healthcare.
- Patients may **lack health insurance**.

### Language and Communication
- Patients may not be able to communicate with the healthcare provider due to a **communication barrier**.
- Even with **interpreters**, communication may be difficult.

## What Is Culturally Appropriate Palliative Care?

Care that uses methods and strategies that improve the quality of life of patients/families living with life-threatening illness which are **generally acceptable and conform to their beliefs, values, and practices**.

Culturally sensitive aspects include:

- The way you **communicate** with client/family
- The **nature of the service**
- The **model of care**
- The **kind of treatment** given

## Cultural Assessment Tools

A cultural assessment should explore:

- **Lifestyle, values, norms, and expressions**
- **Taboos, myths, misconceptions**
- **Diversities / social class**
- **Folk/traditional healing systems** and complementary therapies
- **Structure of service** — who provides care, when, and how
- **Patterns of health and illness**
- **Racism and prejudices**
- **Language**

## How We Can Understand and Support the Dying Person's Cultural Needs

- **Get to know the culture** of the individual/family.
- **Stick to the cultural rules**.
- **Support the person to perform and complete the cultural rituals**.
- **Use appropriate language and terminology**.
- **Accept your own limitations**.

## Ghanaian Cultural Considerations

Areas to meditate on in a Ghanaian palliative care context:

1. **Ghanaian cultural concerns** about death, dying, and bereavement practices
2. **Widowhood rites**
3. **Childlessness**
4. **Religious rites**
5. **Traditional beliefs**, etc.

## Summary

| Component | Focus | Sample Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Cultural Awareness** | Self-knowledge | Examine your own biases |
| **Cultural Knowledge** | Learn about others | Read, study, use cultural pocket guides |
| **Cultural Skill** | Assess effectively | Develop cultural assessment competency |
| **Cultural Encounter** | Direct experience | Attend ceremonies, visit communities |
