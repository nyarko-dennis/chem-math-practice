# Role of the Hospice and Palliative Care Nurse

A nurse doesn't just have a single role in palliative care. Instead, it is a **series of roles** geared towards providing support to both patients and families during one of their most difficult times.

## 1. Role as a Teacher

Nurses are **health educators**. They:

- Teach patients about their health conditions
- Help patients establish skills they can use to manage the situation
- Educate family caregivers on care delivery at home (e.g., medication schedules)

## 2. Role as a Counselor

Patients who are at the last stages of their lives need all the support they can get. Nurses are not only skilled in providing care but are also considered the best when it comes to providing **emotional support**.

They can:

- Encourage patients to **verbalize their feelings and concerns**
- Be open about the things they worry about **without making them feel judged**
- Provide a compassionate, non-judgmental presence

## 3. Role as a Caregiver

Nurses are considered the **primary caregivers** of their patients. When a nurse is assigned to a patient, they are essentially responsible for making sure the patient is physically well.

This role includes:

- Assessing pain levels
- Giving appropriate medications
- Providing non-pharmacological pain relief
- Feeding
- Assisting in **activities of daily living** when the patient is no longer capable of self-care

## 4. Role as an Advocate

This is probably **one of the most challenging roles** nurses have in palliative care.

Most family members and healthcare providers choose aggressive treatment when it comes to acute conditions in an attempt to help patients. Unfortunately, this is not always what patients want.

During these times, nurses are expected to:

- **Advocate for their patients** and speak up for them
- Help ensure the patient's wishes are respected
- Defend the patient's right to dignity, comfort, and autonomy

## Role in Pain Management Specifically

The nurse may help to relieve pain by:

1. Administering pain-relieving interventions, both **pharmacological and non-pharmacological**.
2. **Assessing the effectiveness** of the interventions rendered and the presence of any adverse effects.
3. **Assessing pain** to identify goals for managing pain and how these goals will be accomplished.
4. **Teaching the patient** about pain and strategies to relieve pain, and explaining how pain can be controlled.
5. Meeting the patient's **physical self-care needs**, as patients in pain may be unable to perform their usual activities of daily living.
