# Types of Pain

Pain can be categorized in several ways. Most commonly, pain is categorized by:

- **Duration** — acute vs. chronic
- **Underlying pathology** — nociceptive vs. neuropathic

## By Duration

### Acute Pain

- Relatively **brief duration**: hours, days, weeks, or a few months
- Serves as a **warning** that something is wrong
- Generally viewed as a **time-limited experience**

### Chronic Pain

- **Worsens and intensifies** with the passage of time
- Persists for extended periods — months, years, or a lifetime
- No longer serves a warning function; becomes its own problem

## By Underlying Pathology

### A. Nociceptive Pain

This type of pain is produced when **pain receptors (nociceptors) are stimulated**, affecting somatic and visceral structures.

Nociceptive pain is divided into **somatic** and **visceral** subtypes.

#### Somatic Pain

Somatic pain is further categorized as **superficial** or **deep**.

**Superficial somatic pain:**
- Arises from skin, mucous membranes, and subcutaneous tissues
- Described as **sharp, burning, or prickly**

**Deep somatic pain:**
- Originates in bone, joint, muscle, skin, or connective tissue
- Described as **deep, aching, or throbbing**

#### Visceral Pain

- Comes from **nociceptors in internal organs and the lining of body cavities** (e.g., thoracic and abdominal cavities)
- Visceral nociceptors respond to **inflammation, stretching, and ischemia**
- Examples: pain from a surgical incision, pancreatitis, inflammatory bowel disease

### B. Neuropathic Pain

Neuropathic pain is caused by **damage to peripheral nerves or structures in the CNS**.

**Typical descriptors:**

- Numbing
- Hot, burning
- Shooting, stabbing
- Sharp
- Electric shock–like

It can be **sudden, intense, short-lived, or lingering**. Paroxysmal firing of injured nerves is responsible for the shooting and electric shock–like sensations.

**Common causes of neuropathic pain:**

- Trauma
- Metabolic diseases (e.g., diabetes mellitus)
- Alcoholism
- Infections of the nervous system (e.g., herpes zoster, HIV)
- Tumors
- Toxins
- Neurologic diseases (e.g., multiple sclerosis)

## Summary Table

| Type | Source | Typical Descriptors | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Superficial somatic** | Skin, mucosa, subcutaneous tissue | Sharp, burning, prickly | Skin burn, paper cut |
| **Deep somatic** | Bone, joint, muscle, connective tissue | Deep, aching, throbbing | Fracture, arthritis |
| **Visceral** | Internal organs, body-cavity lining | Cramping, deep, poorly localized | Pancreatitis, IBD, post-surgical |
| **Neuropathic** | Peripheral or central nerve damage | Burning, shooting, stabbing, electric | Diabetic neuropathy, post-herpetic neuralgia |
| **Acute** | Recent injury/illness | Time-limited; warning signal | Post-op pain |
| **Chronic** | Persistent or progressive condition | Lasts months–years | Cancer pain, chronic back pain |
