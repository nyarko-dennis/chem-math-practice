# Adjuvant Analgesics & Breakthrough Pain

## Adjuvant Analgesics

**Adjuvant analgesics** are drugs that were **not originally designed as analgesics**, but which may help in certain kinds of pain alongside standard analgesics.

They can be **started at any step** of the WHO analgesic ladder.

### Commonly Used Adjuvants

| Class | Examples | Pain Type Helped |
|---|---|---|
| **Corticosteroids** | Dexamethasone, prednisolone (1 mg dexamethasone ≈ 7 mg prednisolone) | Pain from severe **swelling or inflammation** |
| **Tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs)** | Amitriptyline, imipramine | **Neuropathic pain** (nerve damage pain) |
| **Anticonvulsants** | Valproate, gabapentin, carbamazepine, phenytoin | **Neuropathic pain** (nerve damage pain) |
| **Benzodiazepines** | Diazepam, lorazepam | **Skeletal muscle spasm** |
| **Anticholinergics** | Hyoscine butylbromide | **Smooth muscle spasm** (e.g., abdominal colic) |

### When to Reach for an Adjuvant

- The pain has a **specific mechanism** (e.g., nerve damage, inflammation, spasm) where standard analgesics alone are insufficient.
- The patient has reached Step 3 of the WHO ladder but pain is still poorly controlled.
- Specific symptoms accompany the pain (e.g., spasm, swelling, burning/shooting quality).

## Breakthrough Pain

**Breakthrough pain** is a sudden flare of pain that occurs **before the next scheduled dose** of regular analgesia is due — when the previous dose has worn off or pain has temporarily intensified.

### How to Manage Breakthrough Pain

Give an **extra dose** of medication when breakthrough pain occurs, **without disrupting the regular schedule**.

**Example:**

| Time | Event |
|---|---|
| 8:00 AM | Patient takes scheduled dose |
| 12:00 noon | Next dose is due |
| **9:30 AM** | **Pain returns → give an extra (rescue) dose** |
| 12:00 noon | **Still give the regular scheduled dose** as planned |

### Dosing the Rescue Dose

- Typically, the **rescue dose is half (½) of the regular scheduled dose**.
- For example, if the regular dose is 10 mg morphine, the breakthrough dose is **5 mg**.

### What If Breakthrough Pain Is Frequent?

If breakthrough pain occurs **repeatedly**, this signals that the **baseline analgesic dose is inadequate**. In that case:

1. **Increase the regular scheduled dose**, or
2. **Move up the WHO analgesic ladder** to a stronger analgesic.

## Key Reminders

- Adjuvants do **not replace** standard analgesics — they **complement** them.
- Always treat breakthrough pain promptly; do **not** wait for the next scheduled dose.
- Reassess pain regularly to determine if the baseline regimen needs adjustment.
