# Death and Dying

> *"Of all human experiences, none is more overwhelming in its implications than death. Yet, for most of us, death remains a shadowy figure whose presence is only vaguely acknowledged."* — DeSpelder and Strickland (2001, p. 5)

## Historical Context

- Earlier in the **20th century**, many people died **in their homes**, surrounded by family and loved ones.
- Later, it became more common to die **in a hospital or nursing home**.
- In the **1970s**, the **hospice movement** began to promote care of dying clients at home or in hospice settings, providing a more dignified and supportive climate.
- Increased technology and aggressive treatment have, in some ways, distorted the reality of dying and death for healthcare providers. For some, **death signifies a failure** to save lives and act as healers.

## Reframing Death

Death, however, is a **natural and universal experience** — a part of life and a component of healthcare.

Healthcare providers must:

- **Acknowledge death as the final stage of growth and development** (Kübler-Ross, 1975)
- **Explore their own mortality** and feelings about dying and death

This is the only way that they can then provide care and comfort to dying clients and their families.

## Definitions

### Death

**Death** is an *event* — the cessation of all physical and chemical processes that invariably occur in all living organisms.

### Indications of Death

- **Absolute unresponsiveness** to externally applied stimuli
- **Cessation of movement and breathing** (including no spontaneous breathing for 3 minutes after artificial respiration has stopped)
- **Complete absence of reflexes**
- **Flat electroencephalographic (EEG) reading** at the time of apparent death

### Dying

**Dying** is a *process* that begins when a person has an untreatable disorder and inevitably ends in death.

In contrast to accidental death, a **terminal illness** is one in which:

- There is **no possibility for a cure**
- The patient's **physical condition declines**
- It ultimately ends in death

## The Dying Process

Patients with terminal illness **progress through the failure of one or more body systems** until death occurs.

The dying process and eventual death may be **peaceful** as the patient:

- Solidifies relationships
- Says goodbye to loved ones
- Resolves unfinished business

## Shifting Goals of Care

When a patient is dying, the **goals of care change**:

| Phase | Primary Goal |
|---|---|
| Earlier illness | **Curative** — extend life, treat disease |
| End of life | **Palliative** — help patient live comfortably; emotional and spiritual support for patient and family |

The dying patient must cope with:

- A declining physical condition
- Relevant issues about existence, spirituality, and separation
- The need for help to compensate for loss of function and maintain quality of life

## Where People Die

- Some terminally ill clients spend their last days in an **acute care setting**, using the best technology and resources available.
- Others prefer to be **at home**, with or without assistance from hospice home care.
- Others choose a **hospice or extended care facility**.
- **Culture and family tradition** may influence these choices.
