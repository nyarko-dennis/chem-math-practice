# Ethics of Care in Palliative Care

When caring for your patient and their family, you must ensure that you **promote core ethical principles**. The fact that a patient is near death does **not** mean they do not deserve respectful and safe care.

> **Presence is everything when end of life is approaching. "Watch" with your patient.**

## Core Ethical Principles

### Autonomy
Respect the patient's right to make decisions about their own care, including the decision to accept or refuse treatment.

### Communication
Open, honest, and compassionate communication with patient and family. Information must be shared in a way that supports informed decision-making.

### Beneficence
Act in the best interest of the patient — prioritize their well-being, comfort, and quality of life.

### Nonmaleficence
"Do no harm." Avoid actions or interventions that cause unnecessary suffering. In palliative care, this includes avoiding treatments that prolong life at the cost of dignity or comfort.

### Justice
Provide fair and equitable access to palliative care services, regardless of the patient's background, socioeconomic status, or condition.

### Confidentiality
Protect the patient's personal and medical information.

### Veracity (Truth-Telling)
Be truthful with the patient and family. Truth maintains trust and allows patients to plan and prepare. (See [Communication](19-communication.md) for details on truth-telling.)

### Privacy
Respect the patient's right to privacy in physical care, conversations, and personal matters.

### Consent
Seek informed consent for interventions. Even patients near death retain the right to consent to or refuse care.

## Summary

| Principle | What it requires of the nurse |
|---|---|
| Autonomy | Respect patient choices |
| Communication | Be open, honest, compassionate |
| Beneficence | Act in patient's best interest |
| Nonmaleficence | Avoid causing harm |
| Justice | Provide equitable care |
| Confidentiality | Protect patient information |
| Veracity | Tell the truth |
| Privacy | Respect personal boundaries |
| Consent | Obtain informed agreement |
