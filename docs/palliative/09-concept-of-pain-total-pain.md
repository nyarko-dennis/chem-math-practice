# Concept of Pain & Total Pain

> *"To Endure Pain Is Unnecessary."* — Arthur Rosenfeld

## Why Pain Matters

Over **70% of people with advanced cancer, HIV disease, or any terminal illness experience pain**.

- Some pains are **short-term**, such as those caused by HIV-related opportunistic infections.
- Many pains associated with advanced cancer and HIV are **long-term** and may get worse over time.

## Defining Pain

Pain is fundamentally a **subjective phenomenon** — the patient's **self-report is the single most reliable indicator** of pain.

> *"Pain is whatever the experiencing person says it is, existing whenever they say it does."*

Although relying on the patient's self-report is essential, this view is problematic for many patients. For example:

- Patients who are comatose or who suffer from dementia
- Patients who are mentally disabled
- Patients with expressive aphasia
- Pre-verbal children

These groups possess varying abilities to report pain. In these instances, the nurse must incorporate **nonverbal information**, such as behaviors, into the pain assessment.

## The Concept of "Total Pain"

The key to palliative care is to provide a safe way for the individual to address their **total pain** — a concept first developed by **Dame Cicely Saunders**.

Dame Cicely Saunders described pain as an overlapping concept involving the **physical, psychological, social, cultural, and spiritual** dimensions.

### Physical Pain

Pain due to **actual or potential tissue damage**. It manifests alongside other symptoms such as:

- Difficulty in sleeping
- Tiredness
- Vomiting
- Cough

### Psychological Pain

Manifested with emotions such as:

- Depression
- Anxiety
- Anger
- Disappointment
- Fear

### Social Pain

Pain related to interpersonal relations, including:

- Lack of social support
- Feelings of being a burden
- Family problems
- Stigma
- Financial and legal issues

### Spiritual Pain

Results from feeling that **life is not meaningful**. Patient/family may ask questions such as:

- *"What is there to live for?"*
- *"Will I go to heaven when I die?"*
- *"Is it possible to be healed by God?"*

There is regret, guilt, self-blame (due to the perception of having offended God), and unresolved religious questions.

### Cultural Pain

Reflects the challenges patients experience due to:

- Language barriers
- Cultural insensitivity
- Need for lifestyle adjustment that affects beliefs and values about life
- Lack of opportunity for cultural expression
- Stigma and discrimination

## Suffering

**Suffering** occurs when people perceive a *"threat to their continued existence — not merely to their lives but their integrity as persons"* (Cassell, 1991, p. 35).

Suffering is a **more expansive concept than pain**. It goes beyond unpleasant sensations or distressing symptoms to encompass the:

- **Anguish**
- **Terror**
- **Hopelessness**

that dying patients may experience.

## Summary: The Five Dimensions of Total Pain

| Dimension | Examples |
|---|---|
| Physical | Tissue damage, sleep loss, fatigue, vomiting, cough |
| Psychological | Depression, anxiety, anger, fear |
| Social | Isolation, family problems, financial stress, stigma |
| Spiritual | Loss of meaning, guilt, religious doubt |
| Cultural | Language barriers, cultural insensitivity, discrimination |
